# Product Marketing by Situation

Map your current challenge to the frameworks that will help you solve it.

---

## Situation 1: Launching a New Product

**The Challenge:** You've built a new product or feature and need to take it to market. You need positioning, messaging, sales enablement, and a coordinated launch.

**Apply These Frameworks (In Order):**

1. **Complete Product Experience Framework** (`Category 1`)
   - *Why:* Define what "launch success" means across all customer touchpoints
   - *Do this first:* Ensure all teams understand the positioning before launch

2. **Segmentation–Targeting–Positioning Framework** (`Category 1`)
   - *Why:* Identify who should buy this product and how to position it for them
   - *Do this second:* Validate that you're targeting the right segment

3. **Value Proposition Canvas** (`Category 2`)
   - *Why:* Map the product's features to the target segment's jobs, pains, and gains
   - *Do this third:* Ensure feature set matches what the segment actually needs

4. **Geoffrey Moore's Positioning Statement Framework** (`Category 2`)
   - *Why:* Create a clear, tested positioning statement to guide all launch messaging
   - *Do this fourth:* Build consensus across product, sales, and marketing teams

5. **Complete GTM Workflow Stages** (`Category 4`)
   - *Why:* Sequence all launch activities across foundation, launch, scale, and optimise phases
   - *Do this parallel:* Run launch planning in parallel with positioning work

6. **Go-to-Market Motion Framework** (`Category 5`)
   - *Why:* Coordinate messaging, content, sales enablement, and customer communication across channels
   - *Do this during launch:* Execute simultaneously across all touchpoints

7. **Feature Adoption Framework** (`Category 5`)
   - *Why:* After launch, drive customer adoption through campaigns and enablement
   - *Do this post-launch:* Measure and iterate on adoption rates

**Success Metrics:**
- Positioning statement approved and socialised across teams ✓
- Launch-week traffic / pipeline / customer acquisition meets target ✓
- Sales team trained and using messaging (80%+ training completion) ✓
- Feature adoption rate reaches 25%+ within 4 weeks ✓

## Situation 2: Entering a New Market or Segment

**The Challenge:** You're expanding into a new geography, industry, or customer size. You don't yet know if your current positioning and messaging will resonate.

**Apply These Frameworks (In Order):**

1. **Product Development Stage Framework** (`Category 1`)
   - *Why:* Conduct discovery to validate that the market actually has the problem you're solving
   - *Do this first:* Interview 15–20 prospects in the new segment before investing heavily

2. **Segmentation–Targeting–Positioning Framework** (`Category 1`)
   - *Why:* Segment the new market and choose your initial target within it
   - *Do this second:* Decide which customer archetype is your beachhead

3. **April Dunford's 5-Component Positioning Canvas** (`Category 2`)
   - *Why:* Test multiple positioning hypotheses for the new segment
   - *Do this third:* Validate that your current positioning works or refine it for the segment

4. **Jobs-to-be-Done Positioning Framework** (`Category 2`)
   - *Why:* Understand what "job" customers in this segment are trying to do
   - *Do this parallel:* Use JTBD to sense-check your positioning hypothesis

5. **Bowman's Strategic Clock** (`Category 3`)
   - *Why:* Map competitive positions in the new market and choose your strategy
   - *Do this:* Identify white space in the competitive landscape

6. **Complete GTM Workflow Stages** (`Category 4`)
   - *Why:* Plan the phases for entering this market (foundation, launch, scale)
   - *Do this parallel:* Build launch plan in tandem with positioning validation

7. **3-Step Product Marketing Strategy Framework** (`Category 4`)
   - *Why:* If this is a bootstrapped entry, simplify to core research, positioning, execution
   - *Do this if under-resourced:* Focus ruthlessly on what's essential

8. **Win / Loss Analysis Framework** (`Category 6`)
   - *Why:* After initial launch, analyse deals to understand why you're winning or losing
   - *Do this after 3–6 months:* Iterate positioning based on real market feedback

**Success Metrics:**
- Discovery interviews show 70%+ agreement on core pain ✓
- Positioning validated with 10+ target prospects (80%+ resonance) ✓
- CAC payback <18 months within 6 months of launch ✓
- Competitive win rate >20% in the new segment ✓

## Situation 3: Competing Against Established Players

**The Challenge:** You're entering a market dominated by one or more incumbents. You can't out-spend them, so you need to find a defensible positioning and differentiate ruthlessly.

**Apply These Frameworks (In Order):**

1. **Competitive Intelligence & Positioning Update Framework** (`Category 7`)
   - *Why:* Map exactly what the incumbents own and where white space exists
   - *Do this first:* Understand competitor positioning, messaging, pricing, and customer perception

2. **Bowman's Strategic Clock** (`Category 3`)
   - *Why:* Plot your position and theirs on the price-value map; identify white space
   - *Do this second:* Choose a defensible strategic position

3. **Product Differentiation Strategy Framework** (`Category 3`)
   - *Why:* Articulate what makes you uniquely valuable given the competitive landscape
   - *Do this third:* Build from your strengths, not by copying competitors

4. **Jobs-to-be-Done Positioning Framework** (`Category 2`)
   - *Why:* Reframe the market around the job customers need done, not competitor categories
   - *Do this if:* Incumbents own the traditional category; JTBD allows you to reframe

5. **Value Proposition Canvas** (`Category 2`)
   - *Why:* Map your differentiation to what your target segment actually values
   - *Do this:* Ensure your differentiation matters to customers, not just on paper

6. **April Dunford's 5-Component Positioning Canvas** (`Category 2`)
   - *Why:* Test positioning that makes competitors irrelevant or positions them as the status quo
   - *Do this:* Validate that your differentiation strategy resonates

7. **10-Step PMM Process** (`Category 4`)
   - *Why:* Build a complete competitive battlecard and sales enablement programme
   - *Do this:* Equip sales with specific competitor responses and differentiation scripts

8. **Win / Loss Analysis Framework** (`Category 6`)
   - *Why:* Analyse why you're winning against incumbents and losing to others
   - *Do this monthly:* Refine messaging based on what's working in market

**Success Metrics:**
- White space identified and verified with customer interviews ✓
- Win rate against primary incumbent >15% ✓
- Sales can articulate differentiation without referring to competitor pricing ✓
- Messaging differentiates you from incumbent (tested with prospects) ✓

## Situation 4: Reducing Customer Churn

**The Challenge:** Customers are leaving faster than you'd like. You need to understand why and fix it across product, messaging, support, and sales.

**Apply These Frameworks (In Order):**

1. **Net Promoter Score (NPS) & Feedback Loop Framework** (`Category 6`)
   - *Why:* Identify which customer segments are churning and what's driving the sentiment
   - *Do this first:* Segment detractors by company size, industry, use case, tenure

2. **Win / Loss Analysis Framework** (`Category 6`)
   - *Why:* Interview recent churners to understand the root cause
   - *Do this second:* Was it unmet expectations? Competitor? Poor onboarding? Missing features?

3. **Customer Onboarding Maturity Framework** (`Category 6`)
   - *Why:* Analyse if customers are getting stuck during onboarding
   - *Do this if:* Early-tenure churn is the problem (customers leaving in first 3 months)

4. **Feature Adoption Framework** (`Category 5`)
   - *Why:* Analyse if customers are using the full value of the product
   - *Do this if:* Customers leave despite using the product initially

5. **Complete Product Experience Framework** (`Category 1`)
   - *Why:* Identify misalignment between what you promise (in marketing, sales) and what customers experience
   - *Do this if:* Churn indicates expectation-setting issues

6. **PMM Lifecycle Management Framework** (`Category 5`)
   - *Why:* Redesign messaging and engagement across the customer lifecycle
   - *Do this:* Ensure you're actively driving adoption, expansion, and advocacy; not just acquisition

7. **Product Differentiation Strategy Framework** (`Category 3`)
   - *Why:* If churn is due to competition, refine what makes you defensibly different
   - *Do this if:* Customers are leaving for competitors, not just churning outright

**Success Metrics:**
- Root causes of churn identified (e.g., 40% onboarding friction, 30% competitive loss, 20% feature gap)
- Interventions designed and measured (e.g., onboarding redesign targets 40% improvement)
- Churn rate trending down within 2 quarters ✓
- NPS of retained segment improves ✓

## Situation 5: Enabling the Sales Team for a New Segment

**The Challenge:** You've identified a new customer segment (e.g., enterprise; a new vertical; a new geography), but your sales team doesn't know how to sell to it. You need segment-specific positioning, messaging, battle cards, and training.

**Apply These Frameworks (In Order):**

1. **Segmentation–Targeting–Positioning Framework** (`Category 1`)
   - *Why:* Clarify the segment's characteristics, pain points, and decision-making process
   - *Do this first:* Sales can't sell effectively without understanding the customer deeply

2. **Complete Product Experience Framework** (`Category 1`)
   - *Why:* Identify what "good" looks like across all touchpoints for this segment
   - *Do this:* Set expectations with product, support, onboarding on segment needs

3. **Value Proposition Canvas** (`Category 2`)
   - *Why:* Build a segment-specific value proposition tied to their jobs, pains, and gains
   - *Do this second:* Map your product's value specifically to this segment

4. **Geoffrey Moore's Positioning Statement Framework** (`Category 2`)
   - *Why:* Create a segment-specific positioning statement
   - *Do this:* Have sales memorise and internalise the positioning

5. **Product Differentiation Strategy Framework** (`Category 3`)
   - *Why:* Clarify where you're unique vs. competitors in this segment's eyes
   - *Do this:* Competitors may own different positions in different segments

6. **Competitive Intelligence & Positioning Update Framework** (`Category 7`)
   - *Why:* Research competitor positioning and messaging specific to this segment
   - *Do this:* Build segment-specific battle cards

7. **10-Step PMM Process (Steps 4–7)** (`Category 4`)
   - *Why:* Build segment-specific message architecture, collateral, and sales enablement
   - *Do this:* Create one-pagers, battle cards, demo script, FAQs for the segment

8. **Quarterly PMM Planning Framework** (`Category 7`)
   - *Why:* Plan and resource the enablement work for the quarter
   - *Do this:* Set realistic targets for sales training, collateral creation, measurement

**Success Metrics:**
- Sales team completes positioning and segment overview training (80%+ attendance) ✓
- Sales can articulate segment-specific value proposition in discovery calls ✓
- Segment-specific deal velocity improves (sales cycle shortens) ✓
- Win rate in new segment reaches 20%+ ✓

## Situation 6: Pricing a New Tier or Service

**The Challenge:** You're adding a new pricing tier, package, or service. You need to understand what justifies the price, position it for different buyer types, and avoid cannibalisation.

**Apply These Frameworks (In Order):**

1. **Value Proposition Canvas** (`Category 2`)
   - *Why:* Understand what each customer segment values and their willingness to pay
   - *Do this first:* Don't set pricing based on cost; set it based on perceived value

2. **Segmentation–Targeting–Positioning Framework** (`Category 1`)
   - *Why:* Each pricing tier may target different segments (SMB, mid-market, enterprise)
   - *Do this:* Validate that each tier has a distinct target customer

3. **Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter (PSM)** (`Category 8`)
   - *Why:* Find the acceptable price corridor for the new tier or service through direct customer survey data
   - *Do this third:* Run this before drafting the tier structure if you have no existing anchor price to work from

4. **Value Metric / Willingness-to-Pay Framework** (`Category 8`)
   - *Why:* Choose the right unit to charge for (seats, usage, transactions) and confirm each persona's willingness to pay for it before locking tier prices
   - *Do this fourth:* Run this alongside or just after PSM if your current pricing doesn't scale with usage, or if you're unsure per-seat is the right metric at all

5. **Good-Better-Best (GBB) Packaging Framework** (`Category 8`)
   - *Why:* Structure the tiers themselves; group features by persona and set fences that map to value
   - *Do this fifth:* Draft the tier structure and price ladder, anchored by the PSM corridor and the chosen value metric, before building sales collateral

6. **Bowman's Strategic Clock** (`Category 3`)
   - *Why:* Understand your competitive position on price-value; position new tier accordingly
   - *Do this:* Don't undermine existing tiers with a premium tier that's actually cheaper

7. **Product Differentiation Strategy Framework** (`Category 3`)
   - *Why:* Clarify what differentiates the new tier (features, support, integrations)
   - *Do this:* Ensure the difference is meaningful enough to justify price gap

8. **Geoffrey Moore's Positioning Statement Framework** (`Category 2`)
   - *Why:* Create positioning for each tier that justifies the price to its target segment
   - *Do this:* Frame in customer outcomes, not feature count

9. **Win / Loss Analysis Framework** (`Category 6`)
   - *Why:* Analyse past deals by segment; understand what features drive higher WTP
   - *Do this:* Use win/loss data to inform tier differentiation

10. **10-Step PMM Process (Steps 5–7)** (`Category 4`)
   - *Why:* Build messaging, sales collateral, and enablement for the new tier
   - *Do this:* Sales needs comparison matrices, ROI calculators, migration guides

**Success Metrics:**
- Willingness-to-pay validated with segment interviews ✓
- Tier positioning avoids cannibalisation (measured by customer flow between tiers) ✓
- New tier adoption reaches 10%+ of new customers within 3 months ✓
- ARPU (average revenue per user) increases ✓

## Situation 7: Building a Customer Advocacy Programme

**The Challenge:** You want to turn happy customers into promoters. You need to identify them, understand what drives their satisfaction, and mobilise them for case studies, referrals, and testimonials.

**Apply These Frameworks (In Order):**

1. **Net Promoter Score (NPS) & Feedback Loop Framework** (`Category 6`)
   - *Why:* Identify and segment your promoters
   - *Do this first:* Know who your advocates are and what makes them promoters

2. **Customer Onboarding Maturity Framework** (`Category 6`)
   - *Why:* Analyse what onboarding experience produces high-satisfaction customers
   - *Do this:* Replicate that experience to create more advocates

3. **Feature Adoption Framework** (`Category 5`)
   - *Why:* Understand which features drive the most value (and advocacy)
   - *Do this:* Customers who adopt more features are more likely to be advocates

4. **PMM Lifecycle Management Framework** (`Category 5`)
   - *Why:* Design advocacy touchpoints across the customer lifecycle
   - *Do this:* Recruit advocates at natural moments (milestone usage, renewal, expansion)

5. **Win / Loss Analysis Framework** (`Category 6`)
   - *Why:* Identify what customers value most about your solution
   - *Do this:* Use these insights to brief advocates on what to highlight in testimonials

6. **Competitive Intelligence & Positioning Update Framework** (`Category 7`)
   - *Why:* Brief advocates on competitive positioning so they can speak to differentiators
   - *Do this:* Ensure advocates can articulate how you're different

**Success Metrics:**
- Promoter segment identified and segmented (% of customer base) ✓
- Advocacy programme launched with 10+ active advocates ✓
- 3+ case studies published featuring advocates ✓
- Referral rate from advocates >5% ✓
- NPS of advocate segment >60 ✓

## Situation 8: Recovering from Messaging Misalignment

**The Challenge:** Marketing is saying one thing, sales is saying another, and the product is delivering something different. Customers are confused, sales is frustrated, and conversions are suffering.

**Apply These Frameworks (In Order):**

1. **Complete Product Experience Framework** (`Category 1`)
   - *Why:* Audit all seven touchpoints to identify where misalignment exists
   - *Do this first:* Map what marketing claims vs. what sales says vs. what product delivers

2. **Geoffrey Moore's Positioning Statement Framework** (`Category 2`)
   - *Why:* Rebuild consensus on one clear positioning statement
   - *Do this second:* Get marketing, sales, and product aligned on one message

3. **Win / Loss Analysis Framework** (`Category 6`)
   - *Why:* Understand how misalignment is affecting customer decisions
   - *Do this parallel:* Interview customers to understand the impact

4. **10-Step PMM Process (Steps 4–5)** (`Category 4`)
   - *Why:* Rebuild message architecture and collateral from the consensus positioning
   - *Do this:* Create collateral that sales can trust and use

5. **Quarterly PMM Planning Framework** (`Category 7`)
   - *Why:* Plan the alignment work with explicit milestones and deadlines
   - *Do this:* Make alignment a top-priority OKR for the quarter

**Success Metrics:**
- All teams agree on one positioning statement ✓
- Marketing collateral and sales messaging align (audit confirms) ✓
- Win rate improves (fewer losses due to confusion) ✓
- Sales team confidence in messaging improves (survey: >80% agreement) ✓

## Situation 9: Scaling the PMM Function

**The Challenge:** You've been a solo PMM and the business is growing. You can't do it all anymore. You need to decide what to hire for next and how to structure the team.

**Apply These Frameworks (In Order):**

1. **PMM Team Scaling Framework** (`Category 7`)
   - *Why:* Understand the evolution from solo to specialised team
   - *Do this first:* Decide where to hire based on your biggest gaps

2. **Quarterly PMM Planning Framework** (`Category 7`)
   - *Why:* Document all the PMM work you're currently doing
   - *Do this:* Use as a baseline to identify what new hires should own

3. **10-Step PMM Process** (`Category 4`)
   - *Why:* Build a playbook that new hires can follow
   - *Do this:* Document how you do positioning, messaging, enablement, measurement

## Situation 10: Measuring PMM Impact & ROI

**The Challenge:** Leadership is asking: "What's PMM delivering?" You need to measure impact across positioning, sales enablement, competitive strategy, and customer adoption; and tie it to revenue.

**Apply These Frameworks (In Order):**

1. **Win / Loss Analysis Framework** (`Category 6`)
   - *Why:* Attribute wins to specific messaging or positioning angles
   - *Do this:* Quantify the revenue impact of positioning work

2. **10-Step PMM Process (Step 9)** (`Category 4`)
   - *Why:* Build a complete measurement framework for all PMM work streams
   - *Do this:* Track win rate, conversion rate, sales productivity, competitive wins

3. **Quarterly PMM Planning Framework** (`Category 7`)
   - *Why:* Set measurable OKRs for PMM work; report quarterly on achievement
   - *Do this:* Make measurement explicit from the start of each quarter

4. **PMM Lifecycle Management Framework** (`Category 5`)
   - *Why:* Track PMM impact across awareness, consideration, purchase, adoption, expansion
   - *Do this:* Measure how PMM content and messaging affect each stage

**Success Metrics:**
- Win rate tracked and trending up (e.g., +10 points YoY) ✓
- Sales cycle reduction measured (e.g., 25% shorter cycle after enablement) ✓
- Revenue attributable to PMM initiatives tracked ✓
- Customer segment NPS improvements correlated to PMM campaigns ✓

## Not Sure Which Situation You're In?

**Ask yourself:**
1. What business outcome are you trying to achieve? (revenue growth, market entry, retention, etc.)
2. What's the biggest blocker right now? (unclear positioning, weak sales execution, churn, etc.)
3. Are you working on strategy (longer term) or execution (shorter term)?

Then find the situation above that matches, and follow the framework sequence.

**Still not sure?** Start with the PMM Lifecycle Management Framework (Category 5) or the Quarterly PMM Planning Framework (Category 7) to clarify your priorities and what PMM should focus on.
