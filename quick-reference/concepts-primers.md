## GTM Strategy vs Product Marketing

**Type:** Primer

**Core concept:** Go-to-market (GTM) strategy and product marketing are often used interchangeably, but they are not the same thing. GTM strategy is the company-wide plan for how a product reaches, wins, and keeps customers, spanning pricing, sales motion, demand generation, partnerships, and customer success. Product marketing is one function that contributes to, and often orchestrates, that plan, but it does not own every part of it. Confusing the two leaves PMMs either over-promising on outcomes they don't control or under-owning the parts of the plan, such as positioning and messaging, that are genuinely theirs.

**Key distinctions:**
- GTM strategy is cross-functional and owned by revenue leadership (a CRO, CMO, or CEO), covering product, pricing, channel, sales capacity, and customer success.
- Product marketing is a function that contributes expertise; it has no P&L, doesn't set sales quota, and doesn't decide the product roadmap, though it influences all three.
- PMM often orchestrates GTM execution without owning GTM strategy itself; PMM's process frameworks describe its operational role, not the whole company-level strategy.
- A simple ownership map helps: PMM owns positioning, messaging, launch narrative, and sales enablement content; it contributes to pricing, channel selection, and campaign briefs; it supports but doesn't own sales quota, demand generation execution, and product roadmap.
- A launch plan is one execution of part of the GTM strategy, not a synonym for the standing, company-level GTM strategy itself.

**Where PMM fits:**
- PMM usually holds the pen on cross-functional GTM documents because it sits at the intersection of product, sales, and marketing, but drafting the plan is not the same as owning it.
- PMM authors the parts requiring deep customer and market fluency, and coordinates parts needing other functions' commitment, without being able to unilaterally commit their resources.
- When a launch underperforms, the useful question is which specific piece of the ownership map broke (positioning, channel, sales capacity, or product), not a blanket verdict on PMM's performance.
