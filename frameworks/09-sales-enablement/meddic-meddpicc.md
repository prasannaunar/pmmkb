# MEDDIC / MEDDPICC

**What it is:** MEDDIC is a deal-qualification framework that gives a sales rep a structured checklist for a single opportunity, created in 1996 inside PTC (Parametric Technology Corporation) by Dick Dunkel, working under SVP of Sales John McMahon alongside Jack Napoli, and now widely used across enterprise B2B sales (documented at meddicc.com, "Who Created MEDDIC?", and discussed by McMahon in his book *The Qualified Sales Leader*, 2021): **Metrics** (the quantified business outcome the buyer is chasing, in their numbers, not yours), **Economic Buyer** (the specific person who controls budget and can say yes without needing anyone else's approval), **Decision Criteria** (the formal and informal requirements the buyer will judge every vendor against), **Decision Process** (the steps, approvals, and stakeholders the deal has to clear, in order), **Identify Pain** (the compelling business reason to change at all, distinct from a nice-to-have), and **Champion** (an internal advocate with genuine influence, not just enthusiasm, who wants you to win and can sell on your behalf when you are not in the room). MEDDPICC extends the same checklist with **Paper Process** (the legal, security, and procurement steps a verbally agreed deal still has to survive) and **Competition** (which alternative, including doing nothing, the buyer is genuinely weighing). This is a sales-owned methodology, not a PMM-authored one: sales leadership and revenue operations own the qualification discipline, the forecasting rigour it enables, and the coaching that gets reps to fill in every letter honestly rather than optimistically. **PMM's role is supporting, not operating**: PMM builds and maintains the content that answers several of the letters correctly, so a rep qualifying a live deal has evidenced material to work from rather than having to invent an answer under time pressure. This entry is scoped to that supporting role.

**When to use it:**
- Sales leadership is rolling out or refreshing MEDDIC/MEDDPICC as the qualification standard, and asks PMM what content maps to which letter.
- Forecast accuracy is poor and post-mortems show deals were called "commit" with no confirmed Economic Buyer or Decision Criteria; PMM's Metrics and Decision Criteria content is often part of the fix, not just sales coaching.
- Win/loss interviews show deals stall or are lost because the buyer's Decision Criteria were never mapped against your differentiators, or the named Champion turned out to have no real influence.
- A new sales hire, or a rep new to a segment, needs a fast way to find the quantified value proof (Metrics), competitive positioning (Competition), and decision-criteria comparison content the checklist expects them to bring into a deal.
- Sales enablement is auditing existing collateral and finds it organised by asset type (one-pagers, decks, case studies) rather than by which MEDDPICC letter each asset actually helps a rep answer.

**How to run it:**
1. **Confirm which letters PMM owns content for, and which sales owns alone.** Metrics, Decision Criteria, Competition, and (partially) Champion enablement are where PMM contributes evidenced material; Economic Buyer identification, Decision Process mapping, Paper Process navigation, and Identify Pain discovery are conversations a rep runs live, and PMM does not own the content for those. Getting this split explicit up front stops PMM building material nobody asked for and sales assuming content exists that was never PMM's to produce.
2. **Build Metrics content from real customer outcomes, not internal estimates.** Pull quantified value figures from the same evidence base as the Features-Advantages-Benefits Ladder and Command of the Message: a sourced number ("$1,800 saved per incident," "4 days to same-day approval") a rep can hand an Economic Buyer as proof, not a marketing claim.
3. **Map Decision Criteria content to the buyer's actual checklist, not your feature list.** Use win/loss interviews to learn the specific criteria buyers in this segment actually score vendors against, then build comparison content, often the same material behind the Competitive Battlecard Framework, that answers those criteria directly, criterion by criterion, rather than a generic capabilities overview.
4. **Supply the Competition letter directly from the Competitive Battlecard Framework.** A rep qualifying "which alternative is the buyer weighing, including doing nothing" should be pointed straight at the relevant battlecard rather than having to research the competitor from scratch mid-deal.
5. **Build Champion-enablement material the Champion can use without you in the room.** A named advocate needs something to forward internally: a one-page business case or the ROI summary behind the Metrics content, written so a non-expert can repeat it accurately to their own economic buyer.
6. **Train reps on where to find each piece, mapped explicitly by letter.** Sales enablement should hand new and existing reps a simple index, "Metrics: here; Competition: here; Decision Criteria comparison: here," rather than a general resource library reps have to search by asset type.
7. **Feed qualification failures back into content gaps.** When deal reviews or forecast post-mortems show a specific letter was consistently weak, a Decision Criteria buyers cared about that no content addressed, or a competitor's Metrics claim reps could not counter, treat that as a content brief for the next refresh cycle.

**Cadence & ownership:** Sales leadership and revenue operations own the qualification discipline itself, the coaching that gets reps to fill in every letter honestly, and the forecast calls built on top of it; this is not PMM's cadence to set. PMM owns keeping the Metrics, Decision Criteria, and Competition content current, refreshed on the same quarterly cadence as the Competitive Battlecard Framework and Command of the Message, and immediately whenever a deal review surfaces a letter the current content does not answer well. A quarterly joint review between PMM and sales leadership, checking deal-review notes for which letters most often come back thin, is the mechanism that keeps this a genuine partnership rather than PMM guessing at what sales needs.

**Example:** Fictional enterprise data-integration company Harlow Systems adopted MEDDPICC after a forecast review showed 40% of deals called "commit" in a quarter slipped or were lost, with root-cause analysis pointing to two consistently weak letters: Metrics (reps described the product qualitatively, with no number an Economic Buyer could take to their own CFO) and Competition (reps often did not know which alternative, including an internal build, the buyer was actually comparing against). PMM built two things directly against those gaps: a Metrics one-pager per top use case, each with a sourced figure from an existing customer ("reduced data pipeline failures from 12 per month to 2, saving an estimated 30 engineering hours a month," drawn from three reference accounts), and a battlecard-derived Competition brief covering the two most common alternatives, including a specific comparison against "build it in-house," an option reps had previously had no structured answer for. Sales enablement retrained the team with a simple rule: no deal moves to "commit" in the CRM without a Metrics figure and a named Competition alternative logged against it. Within two quarters, the slip-and-loss rate on "commit" deals fell from 40% to 24%, and deals where reps logged a sourced Metrics figure closed at a 47% rate versus 29% for deals without one, giving both sales leadership and PMM direct evidence that the content gap, not just coaching, had been part of the original forecasting problem.

**Pitfalls:**
- **PMM building qualification training material instead of qualification content.** MEDDPICC coaching, role-play, and CRM discipline belong to sales leadership; PMM material that strays into "how to run a discovery call" duplicates what sales enablement already owns and blurs an accountability line that should stay clear. Recovery: keep PMM's contribution scoped to the specific evidenced content each letter needs (Metrics figures, Decision Criteria comparisons, Competition briefs), and route any request for qualification coaching itself back to sales leadership.
- **Metrics content that restates a feature instead of a sourced number.** "Improves efficiency" answers no Economic Buyer's question; a rep handed that content still has to invent a number under pressure, which is the exact failure MEDDIC was adopted to prevent. Recovery: require a sourced, customer-derived figure before Metrics content ships, using the same evidence discipline as the Features-Advantages-Benefits Ladder.
- **Treating the content build as a one-off launch rather than a maintained system.** A Competition brief written for the qualification rollout goes stale the moment a competitor changes their pitch or a new alternative enters deals, and reps notice within a quarter when their qualification content stops matching what they hear live. Recovery: fold the Metrics, Decision Criteria, and Competition content into the same quarterly refresh cadence as the Competitive Battlecard Framework, rather than treating it as a one-time deliverable for the rollout.
