# Quiz: Complete GTM Workflow Stages

1. A company launches paid demand generation (a Phase 3 activity) before its Phase 2 messaging has hit the 15% conversion threshold. What does the workflow predict, and what should happen?
   - **A.** Stay in Phase 2 and iterate on messaging rather than moving budget into Phase 3.
   - **B.** Move into Phase 3, but spend only half the planned budget until conversion improves.
   - **C.** Proceed into Phase 3 regardless, since paid spend will validate the messaging faster.
   - **D.** Skip ahead to Phase 4 optimisation instead of returning to Phase 2.

   **Correct answer: A.** Trying to run phases in parallel is a named pitfall: launching paid demand generation before validating positioning typically results in high CAC and low conversion, since spend amplifies an unproven message. The recovery is treating each phase's success metric as a hard gate; if Phase 2's threshold isn't met, stay there and iterate rather than moving to Phase 3.

   *Why not B:* Halving the budget still moves into Phase 3 before the gate is met; the workflow calls for staying in Phase 2 entirely until the threshold clears, not entering Phase 3 at a smaller scale.

   *Why not C:* Paid spend does not validate an unproven message; it amplifies whatever message already exists, good or bad, which is precisely why the framework gates Phase 3 behind Phase 2's conversion metric.

   *Why not D:* Skipping ahead to Phase 4 optimisation without first meeting Phase 2's gate compounds the sequencing error rather than correcting it; the fix is returning to Phase 2, not skipping further ahead.

2. A team under growth pressure jumps directly from Scale (Phase 3) to Expand (Phase 5), skipping Optimise (Phase 4) entirely, reasoning that entering a new segment is more urgent than refining the existing one. What does the workflow warn about this?
   - **A.** Require at least one full optimisation cycle before approving budget for a new segment.
   - **B.** Run Optimise and Expand in parallel, splitting the team's time across both phases.
   - **C.** Skip Optimise whenever growth pressure is high, since speed matters more than efficiency.
   - **D.** Run Phase 5 first and return to Phase 4 only if Phase 5 underperforms.

   **Correct answer: A.** Skipping Phase 4 and rushing to Phase 5 is a named pitfall: it spreads resources thin across a new segment without having first improved efficiency in the existing one. The recovery is requiring at least one full optimisation cycle (minimum 90 days of testing) before approving budget for a new segment or geography.

   *Why not B:* Splitting the team across both phases at once still under-resources Optimise's testing, the same dilution the pitfall warns about, just spread over two phases instead of skipping one outright.

   *Why not C:* The framework explicitly treats this skip as a failure mode under growth pressure, not a valid response to it; efficiency gains from Optimise are what make Expand's resourcing sustainable.

   *Why not D:* The workflow's phases build sequentially, each depending on the validated efficiency gains of the one before; running Phase 5 first and retrofitting Phase 4 reverses the intended, gated order.

3. A team stalls in Foundation (Phase 1) for six months, still chasing more certainty from discovery interviews before drafting any positioning hypothesis. What does the workflow recommend?
   - **A.** Cap Foundation at 10 to 12 weeks and force a go or no-go decision using existing data.
   - **B.** Extend Foundation until 90% of interviews describe the same pain, however long that takes.
   - **C.** Skip the remaining Foundation work entirely and move straight into the Scale phase.
   - **D.** Treat the six-month delay as evidence the workflow itself is unsuited and abandon it.

   **Correct answer: A.** Treating phase timelines as fixed rather than as guardrails is a named pitfall: some teams stall in Foundation for months chasing certainty discovery interviews cannot provide. The recovery is setting a maximum, not just a target, for each phase, and forcing a go/no-go decision at that point using whatever data exists.

   *Why not B:* This still leaves the phase open-ended: chasing a specific certainty threshold, rather than capping the phase by time, is exactly the pattern that let this team stall for six months in the first place.

   *Why not C:* Jumping straight to Scale skips Launch's validation work entirely; the framework's fix is capping Foundation's timeline and forcing a decision, not skipping ahead multiple phases.

   *Why not D:* The framework's own guidance treats this stalling pattern as a common, correctable failure mode with a specific fix (a maximum phase length), not evidence the framework itself doesn't apply.

4. A fintech company completes Phase 3 (Scale) and, per the workflow's benchmarks, sees CAC payback at 16 months and win rate at 22% in its core segment. What should happen next?
   - **A.** Proceed into Phase 4, since these figures meet the named Phase 3 success metrics.
   - **B.** Proceed into Phase 4, but wait one more full quarter to confirm the metrics hold first.
   - **C.** Skip Phase 4 and proceed directly into Phase 5, since Phase 3 already looks strong.
   - **D.** Return to Phase 1, since a 16-month CAC payback signals a flawed original hypothesis.

   **Correct answer: A.** These figures meet the Phase 3 success metrics named in the workflow (CAC payback under 18 months; win rate above 20% in the core ICP), which is the hard gate for progressing into Phase 4's optimisation work, exactly matching the framework's own worked fintech example.

   *Why not B:* Adding an extra quarter's wait once the gate metrics are already met is a delay the workflow's gated design does not call for; the metrics themselves, not a further confirmation window, are what trigger progression.

   *Why not C:* Skipping Phase 4 entirely, even with strong Phase 3 numbers, is the named "skipping Optimise" pitfall; the framework calls for at least one optimisation cycle before Expand, regardless of how good Phase 3 looked.

   *Why not D:* A 16-month CAC payback is within the framework's own named "under 18 months" success range for Phase 3, not a signal the original positioning was flawed; nothing here calls for restarting at Phase 1.

5. A company enters an adjacent segment (Phase 5: Expand) using the exact playbook validated in earlier phases, and reaches Phase 3-equivalent metrics for the new segment in five months instead of the original eight. What does the workflow attribute this faster result to?
   - **A.** The positioning skill and operational muscle built during the earlier phases.
   - **B.** The new segment's own faster Foundation phase, unrelated to the earlier passes.
   - **C.** Pure chance, with no causal link to how the earlier segment was built.
   - **D.** A completely different playbook, unrelated to what was learned building the first one.

   **Correct answer: A.** The workflow's own example attributes the faster five-month result specifically to the underlying positioning skill and operational muscle already existing from the first pass through the earlier phases, which is why Expand explicitly calls for using the validated playbook from earlier phases rather than starting from a blank page.

   *Why not B:* This credits the wrong source: the example ties the speed-up to accumulated skill carried over from the earlier segment's full build, not to something newly fast about the second segment's own Foundation work in isolation.

   *Why not C:* The framework explicitly draws this causal link in its own worked example; the faster timeline is presented as a direct benefit of the accumulated operational experience, not an unexplained coincidence.

   *Why not D:* The example specifically describes using "the same playbook," not a completely different one; reusing validated experience is precisely why the new segment moved faster.
