# Category Quiz: Go-to-Market & Launch

1. A pre-Series A startup has one founding PMM, one dominant customer pain point, and cannot afford to specialise across demand generation, content, and enablement. Which framework fits this stage?
   - **A.** 3-Step Product Marketing Strategy Framework, one owner running problem, positioning, and channel.
   - **B.** 10-Step PMM Process, splitting the process across specialised functional owners.
   - **C.** Account-Based Everything (ABX) / TEAM Framework, built for a named, committee-driven account list.
   - **D.** T2D3 Framework (B2B SaaS), mapping revenue multiples to yearly hiring milestones.

   **Correct answer: A.** The 3-Step framework is built for exactly this stage: a small team with one dominant pain point and no capacity to specialise, trading comprehensiveness for a narrow, well-executed plan.

   *Why not B:* This is the right family of tool for a growing PMM function, but the wrong scope for right now: the 10-Step Process assumes distinct owners for messaging, enablement, and demand generation, and is named overkill for a team this size.

   *Why not C:* ABX assumes a small, named universe of high-ACV committee-driven accounts; nothing here indicates that deal profile, and the methodology needs more resourcing than a single founding PMM has.

   *Why not D:* T2D3 requires validated product-market fit and a repeatable sales motion already in place; using it before PMF creates pressure to force growth the market isn't ready for.

2. A growth-stage company ($2M-$15M ARR) has a repeatable sales motion, but conversion rates have plateaued as the company now serves two distinct buyer types with one blended message. Which framework should it move to?
   - **A.** 7-Step Product Marketing Framework, separate positioning and messaging per segment.
   - **B.** 3-Step Product Marketing Strategy Framework, one team owning a single pain point.
   - **C.** Bullseye Framework (Traction Channel Selection), testing channels for one segment.
   - **D.** GTM Motion Model, classifying which acquisition motion the deal size fits.

   **Correct answer: A.** This is the 7-Step framework's named use case: a repeatable motion straining under multiple buyer types, needing separate positioning and messaging per segment.

   *Why not B:* This is the right family of tool at the wrong scope: the 3-Step framework assumes a single dominant pain point and one small team owning the whole motion, and is a poor fit once a second validated segment exists.

   *Why not C:* Bullseye solves channel selection, not multi-segment messaging; the plateau described here is a positioning problem across buyer types, not a distribution problem.

   *Why not D:* The GTM Motion Model classifies which acquisition motion fits a deal profile; it does not build segment-specific positioning for two buyer types within an already-chosen motion.

3. A company wants to decide, before writing any launch plan, whether its $2,000-ACV product with a single decision-maker should be sold self-serve, via inside sales, or through a dedicated field sales team. Which tool answers this first?
   - **A.** GTM Motion Model, matching deal size and buyer complexity to an acquisition motion.
   - **B.** Complete GTM Workflow Stages, phasing the launch once a motion is already chosen.
   - **C.** Launch Tier Framework (Tier 1/2/3), sizing the process a specific launch warrants.
   - **D.** Pre-mortem, a pre-launch risk exercise run shortly before a launch ships.

   **Correct answer: A.** The GTM Motion Model answers this upstream question directly: which of the five acquisition motions fits a given deal size and buyer complexity, before any launch plan is sequenced on top of it.

   *Why not B:* This is the right neighbouring tool at the wrong point in the sequence: Complete GTM Workflow Stages phases a launch once a motion is already understood, and does not itself classify which motion fits a given ACV.

   *Why not C:* The Launch Tier Framework decides how much process a specific launch warrants; it does not determine which acquisition motion the company should run.

   *Why not D:* A pre-mortem is a pre-launch risk exercise; it does not classify acquisition motions or deal profiles at all.

4. The GTM Motion Model has determined that field sales is the right motion for a company's high-ACV, committee-driven deals. The team now needs the actual practice for running that motion; tiering accounts, engaging the full buying committee, and measuring account-level outcomes. Which methodology operationalises this?
   - **A.** Account-Based Everything (ABX)/TEAM Framework, tiering named accounts.
   - **B.** Product-Led Growth (PLG), running the self-serve side of the motion spectrum.
   - **C.** Bullseye Framework (Traction Channel Selection), selecting one channel to fund.
   - **D.** 7-Step Product Marketing Framework, sequencing a general funnel-based launch.

   **Correct answer: A.** ABX/TEAM is the structural mirror of PLG on the field-sales side: it operationalises exactly this motion through Target, Engage, Activate, and Measure.

   *Why not B:* PLG operationalises the opposite end of the same spectrum, the self-serve motion, not field sales; it is ABX's structural counterpart, not a substitute for it here.

   *Why not C:* Bullseye selects a single traction channel within a chosen motion; it does not build the account-tiering and committee-engagement practice this field-sales motion needs.

   *Why not D:* The 7-Step framework is a general funnel-based sequencing framework; it has no account-tiering, committee-mapping, or intent-signal practice specific to a field-sales motion.

5. The GTM Motion Model has instead determined that self-serve/PLG is the right motion for a low-ACV, single-decision-maker product. Which methodology is the ongoing practice for running that specific motion well?
   - **A.** Product-Led Growth (PLG), defining the activation moment and the PQL signals.
   - **B.** Account-Based Everything (ABX) / TEAM Framework, built for committee-driven deals.
   - **C.** T2D3 Framework (B2B SaaS), mapping revenue multiples to operational milestones.
   - **D.** Launch Tier Framework (Tier 1/2/3), sizing an individual launch, not a motion.

   **Correct answer: A.** PLG is the methodology for running the self-serve/PLG motion once the GTM Motion Model has identified it as the fit, covering activation-moment definition, PQL signals, and the in-product upgrade path.

   *Why not B:* ABX/TEAM is the structural mirror for the opposite end of the spectrum, field sales; it is built for committee-driven, high-ACV deals, not this low-ACV scenario.

   *Why not C:* T2D3 maps revenue-scaling milestones to operational requirements across years; it does not operationalise the day-to-day practice of running a PLG motion.

   *Why not D:* The Launch Tier Framework classifies how much process an individual launch warrants; it has no bearing on which ongoing practice runs a chosen acquisition motion.

6. A company is about to spend $150,000 on a paid campaign for a Tier 1 launch bundling a pricing change with a major feature repackaging. Planning meetings have produced total consensus with no dissent raised. Which tool should run before this launch ships?
   - **A.** Pre-mortem, surfacing doubts a confident, unanimous plan may be hiding.
   - **B.** Bullseye Framework (Traction Channel Selection), selecting the ad spend channel.
   - **C.** GTM Motion Model, classifying which acquisition motion the company runs.
   - **D.** 3-Step Product Marketing Strategy Framework, built for an early single-segment team.

   **Correct answer: A.** Unanimous consensus with no voiced doubts ahead of a major, hard-to-reverse launch is a named trigger for a pre-mortem, designed to surface doubts that groupthink or seniority bias would otherwise suppress.

   *Why not B:* Bullseye selects which distribution channel to invest in; it does not surface failure-mode risks in a pricing and feature-repackaging launch before it ships.

   *Why not C:* The GTM Motion Model classifies acquisition motion; it has no role in pre-launch risk analysis for an already-chosen motion's specific launch.

   *Why not D:* The 3-Step framework is built for early-stage, single-segment companies; it is not a risk-analysis tool and does not fit a Tier 1, cross-functional launch at all.

7. A team is planning its quarterly launch calendar and currently runs every launch, from a minor UI tweak to a major new product line, through the same six-week cross-functional process. Which tool should they apply first to fix this?
   - **A.** Launch Tier Framework (Tier 1/2/3), sizing each launch by scope.
   - **B.** 10-Step PMM Process, running a launch once a rigour level is already chosen.
   - **C.** Complete GTM Workflow Stages, phasing an entire multi-month GTM build-out.
   - **D.** GTM Motion Model, classifying which acquisition motion the company runs.

   **Correct answer: A.** This is the Launch Tier Framework's purpose: classifying each launch into Tier 1, 2, or 3 so process, lead time, and headcount match its actual scope, rather than applying uniform treatment regardless of scale.

   *Why not B:* This is the right neighbouring tool answering a different question: the 10-Step Process describes how to run a launch once a rigour level is chosen, but does not itself decide which rigour level a given launch gets.

   *Why not C:* Complete GTM Workflow Stages phases an entire GTM build-out over many months; it is not the tool for deciding, launch by launch, how much process each one deserves within a quarter.

   *Why not D:* The GTM Motion Model classifies which acquisition motion the company runs; it does not size individual launches within that motion.

8. A SaaS company with validated product-market fit needs to define growth targets backward from fundraising milestones and align PMM, sales, and investors on what "on track" looks like at each revenue stage. Which framework provides this?
   - **A.** T2D3 Framework (B2B SaaS), mapping revenue multiples to yearly milestones.
   - **B.** Complete GTM Workflow Stages, phasing a single GTM build-out by calendar time.
   - **C.** Bullseye Framework (Traction Channel Selection), selecting one channel to fund.
   - **D.** Launch Tier Framework (Tier 1/2/3), sizing an individual launch, not a multi-year plan.

   **Correct answer: A.** T2D3 is built for this exact use case: mapping revenue targets (3x, 3x, 2x, 2x, 2x) to required operational milestones, and aligning PMM, sales leadership, and investors on a shared growth-stage vocabulary.

   *Why not B:* This is the right neighbouring scale but the wrong unit: Complete GTM Workflow Stages phases a single GTM build-out by calendar time and validation gates, not the multi-year revenue trajectory investors compare against T2D3.

   *Why not C:* Bullseye selects a distribution channel; it does not map revenue targets to company-wide operational milestones across multiple years.

   *Why not D:* The Launch Tier Framework classifies individual launches; it has no bearing on multi-year revenue-scaling targets or investor alignment.

9. A team has product-market fit but is running five different marketing channels at once with no clear read on which is actually working. Which framework should they apply?
   - **A.** Bullseye Framework, testing channels then concentrating spend.
   - **B.** GTM Motion Model, deciding how the company sells, not which channel to fund.
   - **C.** Launch Tier Framework (Tier 1/2/3), sizing individual launches by scope.
   - **D.** Account-Based Everything (ABX) / TEAM Framework, a committee-driven field motion.

   **Correct answer: A.** This is Bullseye's named trigger: running several channels at once with no clear read on which is working, diffusing effort so none gets tested rigorously; the discipline is testing cheaply, then concentrating on the winner.

   *Why not B:* This is the right neighbouring decision at the wrong layer: the GTM Motion Model decides how a company sells overall, not which specific marketing channel within that motion deserves investment.

   *Why not C:* The Launch Tier Framework sizes individual launches by scope; it does not diagnose or resolve a diffuse multi-channel testing problem.

   *Why not D:* ABX/TEAM is a full go-to-market methodology for committee-driven field sales; it does not address channel-testing discipline for a company running multiple broad channels.

10. A company running the 7-Step Product Marketing Framework is three weeks post-launch, and leadership wants a standing, recurring mechanism, already built into the framework, to review win/loss patterns and feed findings back into messaging before the next quarter begins. Which step already provides this?
    - **A.** Step 6, its weekly sales-marketing sync iterating on win/loss data.
    - **B.** T2D3 Framework, an annual revenue-multiple checkpoint, not a messaging review.
    - **C.** Pre-mortem, a pre-launch exercise framed around an already-failed launch.
    - **D.** Bullseye Framework, re-testing distribution channels after launch.

    **Correct answer: A.** The 7-Step framework already builds this in: Step 6 establishes a weekly sync on pipeline health, lead quality, and messaging effectiveness, iterating on positioning from win and loss data gathered during that sync.

    *Why not B:* T2D3 does have a standing checkpoint, but at the wrong cadence and scope: it tracks annual revenue multiples and operational milestones, not a specific, near-term messaging and win/loss review.

    *Why not C:* A pre-mortem is a pre-launch risk exercise run before a launch ships, using an already-failed premise; it is not the recurring, post-launch review mechanism this scenario calls for.

    *Why not D:* Bullseye addresses channel selection specifically; a general post-launch win/loss and messaging review is not scoped to channel re-testing alone.
