# Quiz: TAM/SAM/SOM Market Sizing

1. A board deck claims "if we win just 1% of the €20 billion European HR software market, we will reach €200 million". The CFO asks the PMM whether the figure is defensible. What should the PMM do?
   - **A.** Rebuild the size bottom-up from customer counts and price, then cross-check.
   - **B.** Find a more recent analyst report, so the top-down starting figure is less dated.
   - **C.** Lower the assumed share from 1% to 0.5%, so the claim looks more conservative.
   - **D.** Keep the figure and add a slide on the company's three-year growth story.

   **Correct answer: A.** Taking a percentage of a large analyst figure produces a number nobody can defend and hides who the customers are. The method builds bottom-up first and uses the analyst figure only as a cross-check.

   *Why not B:* This is the near miss: better data helps, but it keeps the top-down method. A fresher analyst figure is still not built from reachable customers and real prices.

   *Why not C:* Halving the share keeps the same flawed method. The figure is still not connected to customer counts, win rates or sales capacity.

   *Why not D:* A figure the CFO cannot defend undermines the deck's credibility. Boards respond better to a smaller number with clear evidence.

2. A PMM sizes the market for a product that lets small firms automate bookkeeping. They count total current spend on external bookkeepers and call it TAM. A colleague says many firms with the problem spend nothing today. What does this reveal?
   - **A.** TAM was anchored to today's spend, which can miss a market the product expands.
   - **B.** TAM was built correctly, but SAM should now be cut to firms that use bookkeepers.
   - **C.** TAM should be replaced with an analyst figure for the accounting software market.
   - **D.** TAM was too large and should exclude firms that spend nothing today.

   **Correct answer: A.** Products that make a job cheaper or easier can grow a market beyond current spend. TAM should rest on the number of customers with the problem and what they would pay, with a separate case for market expansion.

   *Why not B:* This is the near miss: it moves to the next layer, but accepts a TAM built on the wrong base. Narrowing a mis-sized TAM carries the error forward.

   *Why not C:* An analyst figure for a different category would not fix the anchoring problem. It swaps one top-down shortcut for another.

   *Why not D:* Firms that do the job themselves today often have the problem and would pay to solve it. Excluding them understates the market.

3. A start-up's sales team of four plans to win 25% of its serviceable market within three years, though its competitive win rate is 15% and three larger rivals split the market. What is the main problem with this SOM?
   - **A.** It was set as an ambition and not derived from win rates, capacity and reach.
   - **B.** It is a reasonable estimate, but the TAM above it should be checked first.
   - **C.** It should be set even higher, so the sales team has a stretch target to chase.
   - **D.** It should be dropped, as SOM is not needed once TAM and SAM are sized.

   **Correct answer: A.** SOM is an estimate of what the business can realistically win, based on win rates, sales capacity, reach and competition. Choosing an ambitious share and working backwards turns it into a goal; any stretch target belongs in a separate, labelled number.

   *Why not B:* This is the near miss: checking upstream layers is good practice, but the flaw sits in the SOM itself. Its inputs contradict the evidence on win rate and capacity.

   *Why not C:* Raising an already unsupported number widens the gap with reality. Stretch goals should be separate from the estimate.

   *Why not D:* SOM sets the realistic revenue goal. Without it, plans rest on TAM and SAM, which describe opportunity, not what the team can win.

4. A PMM's bottom-up TAM for a new vertical comes to £60 million, while an analyst report puts the same category at £400 million. What should the PMM do next?
   - **A.** Investigate the gap to find which assumption, yours or theirs, is off.
   - **B.** Adopt the analyst figure and drop the bottom-up model from the deck.
   - **C.** Average the two figures and present the midpoint as the market's TAM.
   - **D.** Keep the bottom-up figure and leave the analyst report out of the case.

   **Correct answer: A.** A gap of more than about two times between bottom-up and top-down estimates means an assumption is wrong. The cross-check exists to catch errors such as a different category definition, a wrong customer count or a wrong price.

   *Why not B:* Analyst figures often define the category differently or include adjacent spend. Accepting them without investigation reintroduces the top-down problem.

   *Why not C:* Averaging two inconsistent numbers hides the error rather than finding it. The midpoint has no basis in either method.

   *Why not D:* This is the near miss: bottom-up should lead, but dropping the cross-check skips the step that catches errors in your own assumptions.

5. A PMM is choosing between two candidate segments in STP targeting. Which output of market sizing is most useful for that comparison?
   - **A.** SAM for each segment, showing the market the product can serve today.
   - **B.** TAM for each segment, showing the total market for the solution type.
   - **C.** A single company-wide SOM figure, used for the annual revenue plan.
   - **D.** The analyst's headline figure for the wider category the segments sit in.

   **Correct answer: A.** SAM narrows TAM to customers the product can actually serve, given features, geography, price and channels. That makes it the right basis for comparing segments the product could target now.

   *Why not B:* This is the near miss: TAM is relevant, but it includes customers the product cannot yet serve. Two segments with similar TAMs can have very different SAMs.

   *Why not C:* A company-wide SOM blends segments together. Targeting needs a figure for each candidate segment.

   *Why not D:* A category-level analyst figure does not separate the two segments. It cannot support a comparison between them.
