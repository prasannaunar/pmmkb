# Category Quiz: Product Experience & Adoption

1. Churn analysis shows a disproportionate number of cancellations happen within customers' first 90 days, and the team suspects new customers are getting stuck somewhere before reaching real value. Which tool should they use first?
   - **A.** Customer Onboarding Maturity Framework, mapping the milestone drop-off.
   - **B.** Time to Value Framework, defining and validating the activation event.
   - **C.** RFM Model, segmenting the base by recency and value.
   - **D.** Kano Model, classifying features by their satisfaction effect.

   **Correct answer: A.** Early-tenure churn is a named trigger for this framework: it maps onboarding milestones, audits where customers get stuck using drop-off data and churned-customer interviews, and designs targeted interventions for the highest-friction points.

   *Why not B:* Time to Value defines and validates the specific proxy metric for "reaching value," but it doesn't audit where in a milestone sequence customers get stuck; Onboarding Maturity is the tool built to map milestones against drop-off data and churned-customer interviews.

   *Why not C:* RFM segments customers by behavioural value and engagement; it is built for an established base with usage history, not for diagnosing where new customers drop off during their first 90 days.

   *Why not D:* Kano classifies features by their effect on satisfaction; it does not diagnose onboarding drop-off points or early churn patterns.

2. Win rates have been declining for two quarters, and it is unclear whether the cause is positioning, pricing, product gaps, or sales execution. Which methodology directly investigates this?
   - **A.** Win/Loss Analysis Framework, interviewing recent wins and losses.
   - **B.** NPS Framework, surveying existing customers' sentiment.
   - **C.** Time to Value Framework, measuring speed to first value.
   - **D.** Customer Onboarding Maturity Framework, auditing the post-purchase journey.

   **Correct answer: A.** This is exactly the named trigger for Win/Loss Analysis: declining win rates with an unclear cause call for structured interviews with recent wins and losses to identify whether positioning, pricing, product, or execution is actually driving the pattern.

   *Why not B:* NPS does probe why customers feel the way they do, but only for existing customers after purchase; it can't explain why a prospect chose a competitor during the sales cycle, which is exactly what win/loss interviews uncover.

   *Why not C:* Time to Value addresses how quickly customers reach value after purchase; it has no bearing on diagnosing declining win rates in the sales process itself.

   *Why not D:* Onboarding Maturity addresses the post-purchase customer experience; it does not investigate pre-purchase win/loss dynamics.

3. A product team is deciding which of fifteen roadmap candidates should headline the next launch and which should just be quietly shipped as bug-fix-level improvements. Which tool helps make that distinction?
   - **A.** Kano Model, classifying each candidate by its effect on satisfaction.
   - **B.** NPS Framework, surfacing feature requests in detractor verbatims.
   - **C.** RFM Model, segmenting customers by engagement and value.
   - **D.** Win/Loss Analysis Framework, explaining why deals were won or lost.

   **Correct answer: A.** Kano is purpose-built for exactly this: classifying each candidate feature as Must-be, Performance, Delighter, Indifferent, or Reverse, which tells the team which features deserve a launch headline (Delighters) versus defensive, low-visibility maintenance (Must-be).

   *Why not B:* NPS verbatims can surface that customers want a feature, but sentiment comments don't classify what effect it would have on satisfaction if built; Kano's paired survey is what tells the team whether a candidate is a Delighter worth headlining or a Must-be that only needs defensive fixing.

   *Why not C:* RFM segments customers by recency, frequency, and monetary value; it says nothing about how a specific feature affects customer satisfaction or where it belongs in a launch narrative.

   *Why not D:* Win/loss analysis explains why deals were won or lost; it does not classify roadmap candidates by their satisfaction effect for launch-sequencing purposes.

4. Marketing can only ever find a customer reference "just in time" for a specific ask, and the same three accounts get approached every time because they're the ones everyone remembers. Which tool diagnoses this?
   - **A.** Forrester Customer Advocacy Model, diagnosing the programme's maturity stage.
   - **B.** RFM Model, identifying which customers are frequent and valuable.
   - **C.** Customer Onboarding Maturity Framework, mapping the early customer journey.
   - **D.** Kano Model, classifying features by their satisfaction effect.

   **Correct answer: A.** This is the Forrester model's named Reactive-stage symptom exactly: advocacy chased only when a specific asset is due, with the same small pool of memorable accounts approached repeatedly and no systematic tracking or segmentation in place.

   *Why not B:* RFM can supply candidate Champions for the advocate pool, but identifying who is behaviourally valuable is not the same as diagnosing or fixing the advocacy programme's own maturity stage.

   *Why not C:* Onboarding Maturity addresses the early customer experience after purchase; it has no bearing on how references or advocates are identified and managed over time.

   *Why not D:* Kano classifies product features by satisfaction effect; it is unrelated to how a company identifies, tracks, or fatigues its pool of customer advocates.

5. A customer scored a 9 on last quarter's NPS survey, but usage data shows they haven't logged in for two months. Which tool specifically catches this kind of mismatch between stated sentiment and actual behaviour?
   - **A.** RFM Model, scoring behaviour rather than stated sentiment.
   - **B.** NPS Framework, the survey that produced the reassuring score.
   - **C.** Kano Model, classifying features by their satisfaction effect.
   - **D.** Win/Loss Analysis Framework, explaining sales-cycle outcomes.

   **Correct answer: A.** RFM is specifically the behavioural complement to NPS: it measures what a customer actually does (Recency, Frequency, Monetary value) rather than how they say they feel, catching exactly this kind of high-sentiment-but-declining-engagement account before the next survey would reveal it.

   *Why not B:* NPS itself is the sentiment side of this exact gap; it is what produced the misleadingly reassuring 9 score in the first place and cannot, on its own, catch the behavioural decline.

   *Why not C:* Kano classifies features by satisfaction effect; it has no mechanism for tracking individual account engagement or recency of usage.

   *Why not D:* Win/loss analysis investigates sales-cycle decisions for prospective deals; it is not designed to monitor ongoing behavioural engagement of an existing customer.

6. A team has been optimising "time to first login" for two quarters with no effect on retention, and suspects they may be tracking the wrong proxy for when customers actually experience value. Which tool addresses this specific problem?
   - **A.** Time to Value Framework, validating the activation event as a true proxy.
   - **B.** Customer Onboarding Maturity Framework, tracking time-to-first-value as a milestone.
   - **C.** RFM Model, measuring ongoing behavioural engagement.
   - **D.** NPS Framework, measuring sentiment after the fact.

   **Correct answer: A.** This model exists precisely to fix this problem: it distinguishes the qualitative "aha moment" from the quantitative activation event chosen as its proxy, and calls for validating that proxy against real customer language and actual retention data, exactly what "time to first login" was never checked against.

   *Why not B:* Onboarding Maturity names "time-to-first-value" as a milestone metric to track, but it relies on the Time to Value Framework's sub-metric breakdown to define and validate what that metric actually measures.

   *Why not C:* RFM measures ongoing behavioural value and engagement across the existing customer base; it does not validate which specific in-product event best proxies the initial moment of value.

   *Why not D:* NPS measures sentiment after the fact; it does not diagnose whether a specific quantitative activation event is the right proxy for a qualitative aha moment.

7. A company wants to know which existing customers most urgently need a proactive save-play right now, based on actual behaviour rather than survey sentiment. Which tool answers this?
   - **A.** RFM Model, flagging the At-risk segment from behavioural data.
   - **B.** NPS Framework, the survey sentiment this scenario sets aside.
   - **C.** Kano Model, classifying features by their satisfaction effect.
   - **D.** Win/Loss Analysis Framework, explaining sales-cycle outcomes.

   **Correct answer: A.** RFM's At-risk segment is built for exactly this: historically frequent, valuable accounts whose Recency has recently dropped, identified from behavioural data as the clearest, most urgent save-play candidates before a survey cycle would ever reveal the risk.

   *Why not B:* NPS is exactly the survey-sentiment signal this scenario says to set aside; it flags who feels positively, not which accounts are behaviourally at risk right now, which is RFM's specific, real-time read.

   *Why not C:* Kano classifies product features by satisfaction effect; it has no mechanism for identifying which individual customer accounts are behaviourally at risk right now.

   *Why not D:* Win/loss analysis explains sales-cycle outcomes for prospective and closed deals; it does not identify at-risk accounts within the existing customer base in real time.

8. A team notices that customers can complete every onboarding milestone in the model's checklist without ever renewing, and wants a rigorously validated, retention-tested definition of what actually predicts renewal, rather than just pairing milestone completion with a retention number. Which tool provides that specific sub-metric validation?
   - **A.** Time to Value Framework, testing candidate definitions against renewal data.
   - **B.** Customer Onboarding Maturity Framework, the milestone checklist itself.
   - **C.** Forrester Customer Advocacy Model, diagnosing advocacy programme maturity.
   - **D.** RFM Model, tracking behavioural value across an established base.

   **Correct answer: A.** This is precisely what the Time to Value Framework supplies: a validated activation event and Core Value threshold, tested against real renewal data, that give the milestone model's measurement step a concrete, evidenced target rather than an assumed "completed onboarding equals success" proxy.

   *Why not B:* The Onboarding Maturity Framework's own pitfalls flag the problem, that milestone completion isn't the same as retention, but it relies on the Time to Value Framework to supply the rigorously validated sub-metric that actually predicts renewal.

   *Why not C:* The Forrester model addresses advocacy programme maturity; it has no bearing on validating onboarding milestones or activation metrics against retention.

   *Why not D:* RFM tracks ongoing behavioural value across an established customer base; it does not validate which specific early activation event or Core Value threshold predicts renewal for new customers moving through onboarding.

9. A support ticket surge cites a capability the team assumed was a rarely-noticed nice-to-have, and separately, this same capability keeps coming up in NPS detractor comments as something customers now expect by default. Which tool's classification has most likely shifted?
   - **A.** Kano Model, reclassifying a feature as expectations rise.
   - **B.** NPS Framework, the detractor comments naming the gap.
   - **C.** RFM Model, tracking behavioural engagement and value.
   - **D.** Customer Onboarding Maturity Framework, mapping the early customer journey.

   **Correct answer: A.** This is the Kano Model's named drift pattern: a feature classified as a Delighter or Indifferent in the past can quietly become a Must-be as customer expectations rise or competitors catch up, and recurring "missing basic" complaints in NPS verbatims are exactly the signal that reclassification is overdue.

   *Why not B:* NPS detractor comments are the signal that a reclassification is overdue, but NPS itself doesn't classify a feature's satisfaction effect; Kano's paired survey is the tool that actually reclassifies it.

   *Why not C:* RFM tracks behavioural engagement and value, not how a specific feature affects satisfaction; it would not surface a feature's shifting classification.

   *Why not D:* Onboarding Maturity addresses the early customer journey; a feature's satisfaction classification drifting over the product's life is squarely Kano's domain, not an onboarding milestone concern.

10. A company's advocacy programme has a formal tiered ask menu and a named owner, but every request is still generated reactively whenever marketing has a specific need, and no one has checked whether reference-assisted deals actually close at a higher rate. Which tool diagnoses the missing piece of the programme's maturity?
    - **A.** Forrester Customer Advocacy Model, naming the missing Embedded-stage piece.
    - **B.** RFM Model, supplying candidate Champions for the advocate pool.
    - **C.** Kano Model, classifying features by their satisfaction effect.
    - **D.** Customer Onboarding Maturity Framework, mapping the early customer journey.

    **Correct answer: A.** The Forrester model's own stage definitions diagnose exactly this gap: a tiered ask menu and named owner place the programme at Programmatic, but the missing lifecycle-triggered requests and unmeasured commercial contribution are precisely what would need to close to reach Embedded, the model's own named next stage.

    *Why not B:* RFM can supply candidate Champions for the advocate pool, but it does not diagnose the programme's structural maturity stage or whether its lifecycle triggers and measurement are in place.

    *Why not C:* Kano classifies product features by satisfaction effect; it has no application to diagnosing an advocacy programme's operational maturity.

    *Why not D:* PMM Lifecycle Management (Category 5) names Advocacy as a stage, but the Forrester model is the dedicated operational build-out for advocacy maturity specifically; Onboarding Maturity addresses an earlier part of the customer journey and does not diagnose advocacy programme structure.
