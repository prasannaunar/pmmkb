# Quiz: Marketing Mix (4Ps and 7Ps)

1. A company launches a premium $50,000 tier. Product, pricing, channel and messaging were each set in a different meeting by a different owner, and the tier now ships through a discount-led reseller programme. What should the PMM do?
   - **A.** Put all the Ps on one page and test each pair against the target segment.
   - **B.** Re-run the pricing study for the tier and leave the other levers as set.
   - **C.** Ask each lever owner to confirm their own decision is final and evidenced.
   - **D.** Raise promotion spend on the tier until pipeline reaches its target.

   **Correct answer: A.** The mix exists to be read across, not just down: a premium price sold through a discount channel is a conflict between levers that no single-lever review will catch. Putting every P on one page and checking each pair against the segment is the consistency check the model asks for before sign-off.

   *Why not B:* The price may be perfectly sound; the problem is that it contradicts the channel. Re-studying one lever in isolation repeats the separate-meetings mistake that created the conflict.

   *Why not C:* This is the right instinct, checking that each P is decided and evidenced, but it only reads down each P. It never reads across them, so a conflict between a sound price and a sound channel survives the review.

   *Why not D:* More promotion pushes more buyers into a mix that does not hang together. Spend amplifies a consistent mix; it cannot fix a contradiction between price and place.

2. A B2B SaaS team set product, price, place and promotion carefully for a new offer. It wins demos, but late-stage deals stall in security review and new customers churn during a slow, self-guided onboarding. What should the PMM change in the plan?
   - **A.** Extend it to the 7Ps and plan people, process and physical evidence.
   - **B.** Rework the messaging to add security proof points to the sales deck.
   - **C.** Cut the price so buyers accept more risk at the security review stage.
   - **D.** Move the offer to self-serve so buyers can skip the security review.

   **Correct answer: A.** The symptoms sit outside the 4Ps: buyers cannot judge a SaaS offer before purchase, so security reports (physical evidence) and onboarding (people and process) do real selling work. Extending to the 7Ps makes those levers planned decisions rather than gaps.

   *Why not B:* This addresses one symptom through promotion, but a security reviewer wants the evidence itself, such as a SOC 2 report, not a slide about it. It also leaves the onboarding process, the cause of the churn, untouched.

   *Why not C:* A discount does not answer a security reviewer's questions or speed up onboarding. It gives away margin on a problem that price did not cause.

   *Why not D:* Changing the channel does not remove the buyer's security requirements; larger customers will still run the review. It swaps one lever for another without planning the levers that are actually missing.

3. A PMM fills in a full marketing mix in one afternoon from the team's opinions, including "Price: $60,000" for a new tier that has never been tested. Leadership wants to sign it off next week. What should the PMM do before sign-off?
   - **A.** Mark each cell as evidenced or assumed and route assumed cells to deeper work.
   - **B.** Validate the price cell with a pricing study and present the rest as it stands.
   - **C.** Present the mix for sign-off and reopen any lever that misses its metric later.
   - **D.** Ask the sales team to vote on each cell so the mix reflects frontline views.

   **Correct answer: A.** A mix filled from opinion gives leadership false confidence that every lever has been tested. Marking each cell as evidenced or assumed, then routing assumed cells to their deeper entries, turns the mix into a map of what is known and what still needs work.

   *Why not B:* Testing the price is the right kind of move, but it stops at one lever. The product, place and promotion cells were filled from the same opinions and would still reach leadership looking evidenced.

   *Why not C:* Quarterly metrics catch a weak lever after the money is spent. Signing off unmarked assumptions now means leadership approves guesses as decisions.

   *Why not D:* A vote collects more opinion, not evidence. Frontline views are a useful input, but they do not replace the pricing, channel and messaging work each cell needs.

4. A new PMM is asked to build the marketing mix for entering the healthcare segment. They start by choosing channels and drafting messaging, but nobody has agreed which healthcare buyers the offer targets. What should come first?
   - **A.** Agree the target segment with STP before filling any lever of the mix.
   - **B.** Test channels with Bullseye first, then set the segment from the results.
   - **C.** Copy the current mid-market mix and change only the promotion messaging.
   - **D.** Set the healthcare price first, then ask product to fit features to it.

   **Correct answer: A.** The mix is set for one offer and one target segment at a time; every lever is judged by whether it fits that segment. Without an agreed segment there is nothing to test the levers against, so STP comes before the mix.

   *Why not B:* Channel testing is valid work, but in the wrong order: channels that perform for an undefined audience pull the segment towards whoever is cheapest to reach, not whoever the offer is built for. Settle the segment, then test channels to reach it.

   *Why not C:* Entering a new segment is a trigger to check which Ps change, not to assume only one does. Healthcare buyers may need a different channel, proof and process from mid-market ones.

   *Why not D:* Starting from price inverts the mix: price should reflect the value a defined segment places on the product. Without a segment, the price is a guess the other levers then inherit.

5. A PMM's mix for a new $35,000 tier has an empty Place cell: the team cannot decide whether the tier should be sold self-serve, through inside sales or through field sales. Which entry should the PMM use to fill it?
   - **A.** The GTM Motion Model, to match deal size and buyer complexity to a motion.
   - **B.** The Bullseye Framework, to rank acquisition channels by testing them in turn.
   - **C.** Message Architecture, to set how each sales channel should describe the tier.
   - **D.** Good-Better-Best packaging, to decide which tier each channel should sell.

   **Correct answer: A.** The question is which sales motion the tier needs, and the GTM Motion Model answers exactly that by matching contract value and buying-group complexity to self-serve, inside, field or partner sales. The mix routes the Place lever to it for this decision.

   *Why not B:* Bullseye is also a Place entry, but it answers a different question: which acquisition channels bring in prospects, such as content or events. It does not decide whether a rep or the product closes the deal.

   *Why not C:* Messaging belongs to the Promotion lever. It shapes what each channel says, not which channel or motion the tier is sold through.

   *Why not D:* Packaging belongs to the Price lever. It decides what goes into each tier, not how buyers of the $35,000 tier reach and buy it.
