# Category 4: Go-to-Market & Launch

## 3-Step Product Marketing Strategy Framework

**What it is:** A simplified approach to product marketing built on three foundational pillars: understanding your customer's problem deeply, articulating your solution's unique value, and executing a focused go-to-market plan. Unlike complex multi-step processes, this framework distills GTM success into awareness of market, clarity of message, and execution discipline.

**When to use it:** Early-stage SaaS companies (pre-Series A) or when you need to reset messaging after product pivots. Use this when your team is small and you can't afford to specialize across demand gen, content, and sales enablement separately. Also effective when market research shows one dominant customer pain point you can own.

**How to apply it:** 
1. **Define the Problem** – Conduct 10–15 customer interviews asking: Why did they buy? What nearly stopped them? What alternative did they consider? Document the core pain in one sentence.
2. **Build Your Positioning** – Map your solution's unique capability to that pain. Create a positioning statement: "[Target Customer] who [specific pain] can [outcome] with [your product] because [unique capability]."
3. **Execute One Channel** – Pick your strongest go-to-market lever (inbound, outbound, or product-led). Master it before diversifying. Track one leading metric: MQLs, outbound conversations, or trial signups.

**Example:** A vertical SaaS company serving healthcare compliance officers identified their core pain: "We spend 40 hours monthly on manual regulatory tracking." Their positioning became: "Healthcare compliance teams can reclaim 30 hours monthly and eliminate audit risk with automated tracking." They executed outbound-only sales to 200 prospects in their ICP, landing 8 customers in 90 days—enough proof for Series A.

**Pitfalls:** Trying to address multiple customer segments with one message dilutes clarity and wastes limited resources. Also, skipping the interview phase and assuming you know the pain results in messaging that sounds generic ("improve efficiency") instead of specific and resonant.

## 7-Step Product Marketing Framework

**What it is:** A comprehensive funnel-based approach that sequences seven discrete activities from market research through customer advocacy. It maps the buyer's journey and assigns specific product marketing work at each stage to reduce friction and improve conversion. More structured than 3-step, less operationally complex than 10-step.

**When to use it:** Growth-stage companies ($2M–$15M ARR) with a dedicated PMM hire or small team. Use when you have repeatable sales motion but conversion rates plateau or sales cycles elongate. Effective for clarifying handoffs between marketing, sales, and customer success.

**How to apply it:**
1. **Market Research & Segmentation** – Profile your ICP by industry, company size, and buyer persona. Document their buying journey and decision criteria.
2. **Positioning & Messaging** – Create value propositions for each segment. Develop elevator pitches, feature-benefit mapping, and competitive differentiation angles.
3. **Content & Education** – Build awareness-stage content (blog, webinar, case study ideas) and consideration-stage collateral (comparison matrices, ROI calculator, demo script).
4. **Sales Enablement** – Create one-pagers, objection handling playbooks, and customer reference lists for each segment.
5. **Demand Generation** – Brief demand gen team on targeting, messaging, and expected conversion rates. Lock in hand-off SLAs.
6. **Sales-Marketing Alignment** – Establish weekly sync on pipeline health, lead quality, and messaging effectiveness. Iterate on positioning based on win/loss data.
7. **Customer Advocacy & Referrals** – Document customer wins, build case studies, and create referral incentives to seed organic growth.

**Example:** A mid-market HR tech company applied this framework. They segmented into two ICPs: "Fast-growth startups" and "Enterprise HR teams." Step 1 identified startups valued speed and cost; enterprises valued compliance and integration. Step 2 created separate positioning for each. Step 3 built startup-friendly blog content and enterprise-grade integrations guide. Step 4 equipped sales with two separate battle cards. By Step 6, their win rates for startups climbed to 32% and enterprises to 18%—enough insight to double-down on startups.

**Pitfalls:** Treating each step as separate projects rather than an interconnected cycle wastes time and creates siloed work. Also, over-investing in content (Step 3) before sales validation of positioning (Step 2) leads to waste.

## 10-Step PMM Process

**What it is:** A detailed, operational workflow that breaks product marketing into discrete, repeatable work streams across positioning, messaging, enablement, and measurement. Designed for larger PMM teams with specialized roles (one person on messaging, another on demand gen support, etc.). Includes explicit checkpoints for feedback and iteration.

**When to use it:** Series B+ companies ($15M–$50M ARR) with 2+ dedicated PMMs and mature go-to-market. Use when you need consistency across regions, multiple product lines, or concurrent campaigns. Essential when onboarding new PMMs or scaling messaging across teams.

**How to apply it:**
1. **Market Sizing & Segmentation** – Quantify TAM, SAM, SOM. Build detailed ICPs with job titles, pain points, and success metrics for each segment.
2. **Buyer Journey Mapping** – Map touchpoints, decision criteria, and key questions at each stage (Awareness → Consideration → Decision → Adoption → Advocacy). Identify friction points.
3. **Win/Loss Analysis** – Conduct 20–30 customer interviews annually asking: What resonated? What held you back? What did competitors say? Use findings to refresh positioning.
4. **Positioning Statement Development** – Draft segment-specific positioning: Target, problem, solution, proof, differentiator. Test internally with sales and leadership.
5. **Message Architecture** – Create tiered messaging: core claim (one sentence), key benefits (3–5), proof points (customer quotes, data, credentials), proof assets (case studies, ROI model).
6. **Collateral & Channel Strategy** – Map messaging to channels (web, email, events, ads, sales). Assign owners. Set refresh cadence.
7. **Sales Enablement** – Create and maintain: one-pagers, battle cards, demo script, FAQs, objection library, customer reference list. Track usage and feedback.
8. **Campaign Brief & Creative Direction** – Write campaign briefs for demand gen and paid teams. Include positioning, key message, audience, success metrics, timing.
9. **Performance Measurement & Feedback Loop** – Track win rate, conversion rate by segment, sales feedback on collateral quality. Publish monthly reporting to leadership.
10. **Iteration & Refresh Cycle** – Every 90 days, review win/loss data, competitive landscape, and product updates. Refresh messaging if significant shifts detected.

**Example:** Atlassian's approach to PMM at scale. Step 1 identified DevOps teams (high-volume, low-ACV) vs. Enterprise IT (low-volume, high-ACV) as distinct segments. Steps 2–3 revealed DevOps cared most about integration speed; IT cared about security and governance. Step 4–5 created two positioning pathways. Step 6 assigned one PMM to DevOps (content, digital), another to Enterprise (sales enablement, case studies). Step 9 showed DevOps collateral had 8x higher engagement. Step 10 shifted 60% of PMM resources to DevOps content, lifting that segment's CAC payback by 6 months.

**Pitfalls:** Over-documentation creates bureaucracy. Assign owners, deadlines, and refresh windows—but don't create approval chains that slow iteration. Also, treating measurement (Step 9) as a reporting afterthought means you'll discover problems too late to fix.

## Complete GTM Workflow Stages

**What it is:** An end-to-end roadmap that sequences go-to-market activities across five distinct phases: Foundation, Launch, Scale, Optimize, and Expand. Unlike framework-focused approaches, this workflow explicitly defines timeline, team ownership, deliverables, and success criteria for each stage. It's designed to prevent common GTM sequencing mistakes (e.g., scaling demand before validating positioning).

**When to use it:** Founder-led companies preparing to hire their first marketer, or established companies launching into new markets or product lines. Use when GTM risks are high (new category, unfamiliar segment) and you need checkpoints to validate before investing heavily. Also applicable when rebuilding GTM after product pivot or major organizational change.

**How to apply it:**

**Phase 1: Foundation (Weeks 1–8)**
- Conduct 20+ customer discovery interviews to validate problem fit
- Document ICP, buyer personas, and buying process
- Draft initial positioning hypothesis and key messages
- Deliverables: Customer research doc, ICP profile, positioning statement
- Success metric: 70% of customers describe the same core pain

**Phase 2: Launch (Weeks 9–16)**
- Build minimal GTM assets (landing page, one-pager, demo script, three case studies)
- Identify one primary go-to-market channel (inbound, outbound, product-led) and validate messaging on 50+ prospects
- Set up basic sales-marketing reporting and SLAs
- Deliverables: Website, sales collateral, operational playbook
- Success metric: 15%+ conversion rate from target segment; sales validates messaging alignment

**Phase 3: Scale (Months 5–12)**
- Expand successful channel with additional capacity (sales hires, paid spend, content production)
- Launch secondary channel after primary channel shows 18+ month CAC payback
- Formalize sales enablement and develop competitive battle cards
- Establish monthly win/loss review cadence
- Deliverables: Expanded collateral library, sales process documentation, competitive analysis
- Success metric: 3x lead volume, CAC payback <18 months, >20% win rate in ICP

**Phase 4: Optimize (Months 13–18)**
- Conduct win/loss analysis on 30+ deals; iterate messaging based on findings
- Test conversion optimization across landing pages, email, discovery calls
- Launch third channel or expand into adjacent segment
- Implement product marketing ops tooling (CRM for win/loss, content management system)
- Deliverables: Refreshed positioning, A/B test results, optimized sales process
- Success metric: CAC payback drops to <12 months; conversion rates improve 15–20%

**Phase 5: Expand (Months 19+)**
- Enter new segment, geography, or product line using validated GTM playbook
- Build dedicated PMM roles for new areas (vertical PMM, regional PMM)
- Automate repeatable tasks; focus on strategic work
- Build customer advocacy program and referral channel
- Deliverables: Segment-specific playbooks, case studies, advocacy framework
- Success metric: New segment achieves Phase 3 metrics within 6 months; organic referral pipeline >20%

**Example:** A fintech B2B SaaS company used this workflow. Foundation (8 weeks) revealed target customers were "payment operations teams afraid of compliance violations." Launch (8 weeks) validated messaging on 60 prospects via outbound; 18% converted to demos. Scale (8 months) added inbound marketing; added 3 new customer stories. By month 12, they had 200 SQLs/month and 22% win rate. Optimize phase (6 months) showed competitors emphasized cost; they shifted message to "compliance peace of mind," improving win rate to 28%. In Expand phase, they entered adjacent segment (treasury teams) with same playbook, hitting Scale metrics in 5 months.

**Pitfalls:** Trying to do all phases in parallel wastes money. A common mistake is launching paid demand gen (Phase 3) before validating positioning (Phase 2), resulting in high CAC and low conversion. Also, skipping Phase 4 (optimization) and rushing to Phase 5 (expand) spreads resources thin without learning.

## T2D3 Framework (B2B SaaS)

**What it is:** A go-to-market methodology designed specifically for B2B SaaS scaling, introduced by SaaS investor Neeraj Agrawal and popularised by venture capitalist Christoph Janz. The acronym stands for "Triple, Triple, Double, Double, Double"—meaning grow revenue by 3x in year 1, 3x in year 2, 2x in year 3, 2x in year 4, and 2x in year 5. It maps revenue targets to required operational milestones and helps PMMs align messaging with each scaling phase.

**When to use it:** SaaS companies (pre-Series B through Series C) with validated product-market fit and repeatable sales motion. Use when you need to define growth targets backward from fundraising milestones or when scaling sales and marketing teams. Effective for aligning PMM, sales leadership, and investors on cadence and required inputs.

**How to apply it:**
1. **Year 1 (3x growth)** – Set ARR target (e.g., $1M → $3M). Required: strong founding PMM messaging, initial case studies, validated sales playbook. Marketing focus: establishing thought leadership, early customer references. Sales focus: outbound-only, land 30–50 customers.
2. **Year 2 (3x growth)** – Set ARR target ($3M → $9M). Required: dedicated PMM hire, expanded collateral library, product-market validation across 2+ segments. Marketing focus: inbound + outbound, expand to paid channels. Sales focus: hire sales manager, systematize playbook, scale to 80–120 customers.
3. **Year 3 (2x growth)** – Set ARR target ($9M → $18M). Required: PMM specialization (one person on demand gen, one on sales enablement), competitive battle cards, regional expansion. Marketing focus: refine messaging by segment, launch events or webinar program. Sales focus: add enterprise sales rep, diversify go-to-market.
4. **Year 4 (2x growth)** – Set ARR target ($18M → $36M). Required: VP of Marketing hire, segment-specific PMMs, mature sales operations. Marketing focus: brand building, field marketing, customer advocacy. Sales focus: hire sales directors, build inside sales team, add strategic accounts. 
5. **Year 5 (2x growth)** – Set ARR target ($36M → $72M). Required: Chief Marketer hire, multi-product strategy, international expansion. Marketing focus: category authority, partner ecosystem. Sales focus: enterprise focus, account-based marketing.

**Example:** A healthcare SaaS company followed T2D3. Year 1: $500K → $1.5M ARR by validating their "compliance peace of mind" positioning with 35 customers via outbound. Year 2: $1.5M → $4.5M by hiring a PMM and building inbound, adding case studies, pivoting to mid-market. Year 3: $4.5M → $9M by splitting into two segments (compliance, operations) with separate messaging, launching webinars. Year 4: $9M → $18M by entering two new verticals with product expansion. Year 5: $18M → $36M by building enterprise sales team and international sales.

**Pitfalls:** Rigidly adhering to T2D3 targets without market feedback wastes effort and burns the team. Use the framework as a guide, not gospel. Also, trying to execute all five milestones simultaneously (hiring, product launches, market expansion) without sequencing leads to failure. Sequence conservatively: validate one segment before expanding to two; scale sales before expanding product.
