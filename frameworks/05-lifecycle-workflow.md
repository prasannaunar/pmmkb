# Category 5: Lifecycle & Workflow

## PMM Lifecycle Management Framework

**What it is:** A model that defines the product marketing role across the entire customer lifecycle: awareness (before customer knows you exist), consideration (evaluating options), purchase (deciding to buy), onboarding (getting started), adoption (using the product fully), expansion (upselling/cross-selling), and advocacy (turning customers into promoters). PMM activities shift at each stage—awareness demands thought leadership and SEO; expansion demands case studies and ROI calculators.

**When to use it:**
- To scope PMM responsibilities and prioritize work across the customer journey
- When defining which customer touchpoints PMM owns vs. sales, support, or customer success
- During quarterly planning to allocate budget and team resources across lifecycle stages
- To identify messaging gaps (e.g., "We have launch messaging but no expansion messaging")
- When a new team member joins; it clarifies where PMM adds value at each stage

**How to apply it:**
1. **Define Lifecycle Stages:** Map your actual customer journey (Awareness → Consideration → Purchase → Onboarding → Adoption → Expansion → Advocacy).
2. **Identify Key Interactions:** For each stage, list the touchpoints where customers interact with your brand (website, sales call, email campaign, in-app tutorial, support article, case study, etc.).
3. **Assign PMM Activities:** For each touchpoint, document what PMM should deliver (messaging, content, collateral, positioning, enablement).
4. **Map Ownership:** Which activities does PMM own? Which does it partner on? Document handoffs to sales, support, or customer success.
5. **Identify Gaps:** Are there stages with weak messaging or missing content? Are you over-investing in one stage and under-investing in another?
6. **Sequence Work:** Prioritize the highest-impact stages first (usually Awareness and Expansion generate the most revenue impact).
7. **Measure:** Define success metrics for each stage (Awareness: organic traffic; Consideration: demo requests; Expansion: upsell win rate).

**Example:**

A B2B SaaS finance tool maps:
- **Awareness:** SEO content (blog, guides), thought leadership, webinars → PMM writes content, partners with product on webinars
- **Consideration:** Competitor comparisons, ROI calculator, case studies, sales enablement → PMM owns competitor research, writes case studies, briefs sales
- **Purchase:** Sales pitch, proposal customization, contract → PMM provides objection handling, positioning training to sales
- **Onboarding:** Welcome emails, in-app tutorials, onboarding webinars → PMM writes welcome copy, partners with support on tutorials
- **Adoption:** Feature tutorials, best-practices guides, customer newsletter → PMM creates adoption campaigns, measures feature adoption rates
- **Expansion:** Upsell messaging, ROI case studies, expansion playbook → PMM identifies upsell use cases, writes expansion-focused case studies
- **Advocacy:** NPS campaigns, referral program, customer advisory board → PMM recruits advocates, coordinates testimonial collection

Success metrics: Organic traffic (Awareness), SQLs from content (Consideration), win rate lift (Purchase), time-to-first-value (Onboarding), feature adoption (Adoption), upsell attach rate (Expansion), NPS and referrals (Advocacy).

**Pitfalls:**
- **Over-focusing on Awareness:** Many PMM teams spend 80% of time on top-of-funnel content and neglect Expansion, where retention and revenue growth actually happen.
- **Unclear ownership:** If PMM doesn't explicitly own onboarding or support messaging, no one will—it falls through the cracks or becomes inconsistent.
- **Forgetting the internal handoff:** Messaging flows from PMM to sales to support. If handoff is poor, the message gets lost or distorted at each stage.

## Go-to-Market Motion Framework

**What it is:** A structured approach to executing a product launch or major campaign across multiple channels simultaneously: messaging, content, partnerships, events, sales enablement, and customer communication. The framework ensures coordinated, synchronized launch activity across teams rather than ad-hoc, piecemeal efforts.

**When to use it:**
- For any product launch, major feature release, or category creation
- When entering a new market or segment
- For seasonal campaigns or timed promotions
- When you need multiple teams (marketing, sales, product, partnerships) to execute in sync

**How to apply it:**
1. **Define the Launch Narrative:** What is the core story you're telling the market? (E.g., "We're solving X problem for Y customer in a radically different way")
2. **Design the Motion:**
   - **Pre-launch (4–8 weeks prior):** Build awareness, create curiosity. Tease through thought leadership, partner announcements, press releases.
   - **Launch week:** Simultaneous announcement across all channels. Website refresh, email campaign, sales kickoff, press coverage, social media surge, customer communication.
   - **Post-launch (4–12 weeks):** Sustain momentum. Content series, case studies, webinars, sales plays, customer testimonials.
3. **Assign Channel Owners:** Website, email, paid ads, content, partnerships, PR, social, events, sales enablement—each channel needs a clear owner and coordination cadence.
4. **Create Dependency Map:** What needs to happen first? Website > email > ads. Sales training > sales outreach. Content > paid promotion. Document the sequence.
5. **Set Sync Points:** Daily standups pre-launch, weekly post-launch to track momentum, call out blockers, adjust messaging if market feedback arrives.
6. **Measure:** Track engagement, clicks, SQL volume, win rate lift, revenue attribution from the motion.

**Example:**

Enterprise data platform launching "Real-Time Analytics" module:

- **Pre-launch:** Thought leadership article "Why Real-Time Matters for Analytics" published on TechCrunch; webinar with analyst firm; press embargo placed
- **Launch day:** Website hero section updated; feature announcement emailed to entire customer base; sales team calls top 50 accounts; social media campaign launches; Slack notification to all employees
- **Week 1–2:** Customer case study; analyst briefing; product webinar; sales playbook distributed; paid ads targeting competitors' customers
- **Weeks 3–12:** Weekly "Real-Time Analytics" customer stories; webinar series; sales contests to drive adoption; ROI calculator promoted to prospects

Success metrics: Launch-week traffic (+200%?), email open rate (>40%?), SQL volume week-of-launch, ASP uplift for accounts adopting feature, customer adoption rate 60 days post-launch.

**Pitfalls:**
- **Lack of coordination:** Marketing publishes a blog post, sales doesn't know about it, support isn't prepared for questions. Momentum dies.
- **Announcement without follow-through:** Big launch event, then silence. Sustaining momentum for 4–12 weeks post-launch is where the real work happens.
- **Ignoring market feedback:** If customers are confused about the announcement or pushing back on positioning, iterate fast. Don't defend the original message if it's not landing.

## Feature Adoption Framework

**What it is:** A systematic approach to driving customer adoption of new or underutilized features. It recognizes that building a feature is different from customers using it—many products languish with powerful capabilities that most users never discover or adopt. The framework applies product marketing discipline to internal product adoption.

**When to use it:**
- After launching a new feature to ensure customers actually discover and use it
- For features with low adoption rates despite strong product-market fit
- To accelerate adoption of features that enable upsell opportunities
- When you notice customers using a workaround instead of a built-in feature
- Quarterly or as new features ship

**How to apply it:**
1. **Understand the Feature:** What problem does it solve? Who should use it? What's the adoption barrier (discoverability, complexity, unclear value)?
2. **Segment Users:** Who has already adopted? Who hasn't? Why not? Segment by persona, company size, industry, or use case.
3. **Create Adoption Campaign:**
   - **In-app messaging:** Prompt users at the moment they'd benefit most (contextual banners, tooltips, walkthrough).
   - **Email campaign:** Targeted messaging explaining the feature and its benefit, with a clear CTA.
   - **Enablement content:** Tutorial video, written guide, webinar, or best-practices documentation.
   - **Sales outreach:** For high-value accounts, personal outreach from customer success or sales.
4. **Measure:** Track activation rate (% who use it once), retention rate (% who use it repeatedly), and business impact (revenue, NPS lift, upsell, retention).
5. **Iterate:** If adoption is low, test messaging, simplify the feature, or provide more training. Don't assume customers will figure it out on their own.
6. **Celebrate wins:** Share adoption success stories and user testimonials to encourage broader adoption.

**Example:**

A project management tool ships a "AI-powered task breakdown" feature. Initial adoption: 5% of users.

- **Research:** Interviews reveal users don't know it exists or think it's "too complex for their use case."
- **Segment:** Power users (>10 tasks/week) vs. casual users; tech-forward companies vs. non-tech
- **Campaign for power users:** In-app banner when they create tasks; email: "Save 2 hours a week with AI task breakdown"; webinar: "How to use AI to stop task planning from taking forever"
- **Campaign for casual users:** Simplified tooltip; email: "Your tasks just got smarter"; optional tutorial
- **Result:** Adoption lifts to 25% within 4 weeks; power-user adoption hits 60%; feature becomes a key differentiator in sales conversations

**Pitfalls:**
- **Assuming "if we build it, they will come":** Customers won't discover features on their own. You need active adoption campaigns.
- **One-size-fits-all messaging:** Power users and beginners respond to different messages. Tailor based on segment.
- **Launching without onboarding:** If customers don't understand how to use the feature, no amount of messaging will drive adoption.
