# Measurement & Reporting Guide

Every entry's quick-reference card already lists **success metrics** (what to measure after applying it). This guide adds the two things the cards don't cover: a **baseline** to capture *before* you apply an entry, so the success metric has something to be measured against, and a **reporting cadence and owner**, so the metric actually gets reported somewhere rather than sitting in a spreadsheet nobody revisits.

**How to use this:** find your entry below, note the baseline metric before you start, apply the entry, then track the linked quick-reference card's success metrics on the stated cadence. The closing section proposes a single roll-up dashboard for the handful of metrics that recur across most entries, so you are not building 55 separate trackers.

**What this is not:** a replacement for each entry's own "Success metrics" list (see `quick-reference/`), a case-study archive (see PLAN.md's Phase 3.1, still open), or the maintenance calendar (see FEEDBACK-LOG.md's Annual Update Schedule, which this guide's cadence column feeds into).

## Category 1: Market & Customer Understanding

| Entry | Baseline (capture before applying) | Reporting cadence | Reported to |
|---|---|---|---|
| STP Framework | Current win rate, sales cycle length, and CAC payback, unsegmented | Quarterly review; ad hoc when a competitor or product change reshapes the market | Quarterly PMM Planning review |
| Complete Product Experience Framework | Current NPS, churn rate, and support SLA adherence | Quarterly coherence check, or immediately after any repositioning | Cross-functional stakeholders (product, sales, support) |
| Product Development Stage Framework | Trial/paid and ARR targets agreed at project kickoff | Reviewed at each phase gate (ideation, prototype, beta, launch, maturity) | Product and launch stakeholders |
| Voice of the Customer (VoC) Programme | Current churn rate, NPS, and share of feedback tied to no actioned theme (typically near 0% pre-launch) | Weekly triage internally; synthesis reported quarterly | Cross-functional decision owners (product, positioning) |
| JTBD Switch Interview Method | Current demo-to-trial conversion rate and the generic reasons (e.g. "better fit") sales currently gives for wins | Run per positioning project, not continuously | Positioning project owner |
| ICP Development Methodology | Blended close rate and blended sales cycle length, unscored | Build once; refresh when win rate or deal size drifts from the model's assumptions (roughly annually) | Sales operations and sales leadership |
| Sean Ellis 40% Test (PMF Survey) | Current usage/activation numbers and growth-spend efficiency (e.g. CAC trend) before committing further spend | Every two quarters, or off-cycle after a repositioning or major release | Product leadership (owns the go/no-go call) |

## Category 2: Positioning & Messaging

| Entry | Baseline (capture before applying) | Reporting cadence | Reported to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Geoffrey Moore's Positioning Statement Framework | Win rate vs named competitors, sales cycle length, discovery-to-demo conversion | Revisit when positioning is contested, or annually | Sales and marketing leadership sign-off |
| April Dunford's 5-Component Positioning Canvas | Win rate against new entrants, average deal size in the target segment | Re-run when the competitive set changes materially | Positioning owner / leadership review |
| Jobs-to-be-Done Positioning Framework | Trial-to-paid conversion and the share of won deals citing a generic (non-job) reason | Revisit when churn or onboarding data reveal an unplanned use case | PMM positioning lead |
| Value Proposition Canvas | Homepage-to-trial conversion and close rate for leads citing the led-with pain, before the canvas exists | Before each roadmap prioritisation cycle, or per segment | Product and PMM roadmap review |
| Message Architecture (Messaging House) | Demo-request conversion, message-test clarity score, and new-hire ramp time before the house is published | Published once; reviewed quarterly as the single source of truth | All go-to-market teams |
| Dunford's 10-Step Positioning Process | Win rate against named competitors and sales cycle length before the process runs | Once per positioning cycle (new market, pivot, merger) | Leadership positioning review |
| Command of the Message | Win rate against the named incumbent and close rate on deals lacking a quantified value figure | Trained and role-played per sales cohort; tracked per deal continuously via CRM | Sales leadership / enablement |
| Features-Advantages-Benefits (FAB) Ladder | Message-test "clear and compelling" score and feature mention rate in sales calls, pre-ladder | Applied per asset as it is written; audited quarterly | PMM content review |
| StoryBrand SB7 Framework | Bounce rate and homepage-to-trial conversion before the rewrite | Applied per major page or script; re-tested annually | Marketing / web team |
| Message Testing (Message-Market Fit) | Existing clarity/compelling score, click-through rate, and cost per qualified lead for the current copy | Before every major asset ships; quarterly or semi-annual re-test for evergreen assets | Marketing leadership sign-off |

|---|---|---|---|

| Entry | Baseline (capture before applying) | Reporting cadence | Reported to |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3-Step Product Marketing Strategy Framework | Time since positioning was last reset; current channel conversion vs industry benchmark | Weekly channel metric; interview-to-positioning turnaround tracked per cycle | Founder / small team weekly sync |

| Launch Tier Framework (Tier 1/2/3) | Current sales enablement completion rate and actual lead time used per launch, pre-tiering | Scored per launch; short retrospective each quarter | Launch calendar owner / cross-functional leads |

## Concepts (Primers)

| Entry | Note |
|---|---|
| GTM Strategy vs Product Marketing | A primer has nothing to "apply" and no success metric of its own (see CLAUDE.md's Content Types). ## Building a One-Page PMM Measurement Dashboard

Most of the 55 baseline/success-metric pairs above roll up into a small set of recurring metrics. Rather than tracking 55 separate charts, build one dashboard around these, refreshed on the cadence shown, and drill into the category tables above only when a number moves:

| Roll-up metric | Fed by | Cadence | Healthy direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Win rate (blended and by named competitor) | STP, Positioning Canvas, Battlecards, Command of the Message, Win/Loss | Monthly | Up, or held against a growing pipeline |
| Sales cycle length | Positioning Statement, Bowman's Clock, Perceptual Map, GTM Motion Model | Monthly | Down, or stable at a larger deal size |
| CAC payback period | STP, GTM Motion Model, Bullseye, Complete GTM Workflow Stages | Quarterly | Trending toward the 12-18 month benchmark |
| NPS (blended and by segment) | Complete Product Experience, VoC Programme, NPS & Feedback Loop, Kano Model | Quarterly | Up, or held above the 30-40 "acceptable" band |
| Trial-to-paid / activation conversion | Product Development Stage, JTBD, PLG, Sean Ellis 40% Test | Monthly | Up |
| Churn / retention | VoC Programme, Onboarding Maturity, Usage-Based Pricing, Feature Adoption | Quarterly | Down (churn) / up (NRR) |
| Enablement content usage rate | Battlecard, Buyer's Journey Content Map, Sales Enablement Maturity Model, MEDDIC | Quarterly | Up |

**Suggested layout:** one row per roll-up metric, three columns (this quarter, last quarter, target), plus a "driven by" note naming which entry from the tables above is currently being applied to move that number. This mirrors the single-page worksheet pattern used throughout the KB's "Apply it" sections, so it needs no new tooling beyond a spreadsheet or BI dashboard.

**Reporting rhythm:** this dashboard's monthly and quarterly cadences slot directly into the cadences already documented in [FEEDBACK-LOG.md](FEEDBACK-LOG.md) (the monthly feedback session and the quarterly one-framework-per-week review) and [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md)'s January/April/July/October annual review calendar; it is the dashboard those sessions should have open, not a separate reporting track.
