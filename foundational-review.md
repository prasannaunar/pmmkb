**Status:** Complete preliminary analysis with findings and recommendations

## Executive Summary

This review validates the frameworks against established sources, checks attribution accuracy, examines example credibility, and identifies quality gaps. Of the 19 frameworks:

- **13 frameworks** are established, industry-recognised frameworks with proper attribution
- **6 frameworks** are internal/custom frameworks (appears appropriate for PMM-specific contexts)
- **1 critical attribution error** found (T2D3 Framework)
- **Multiple minor opportunities** for enhancement (see detailed findings)

**Overall Assessment:** Knowledge base is strong on established frameworks with good real-world examples. Recommendations focus on attribution clarity, example specificity, and cross-framework linkage.

## Detailed Validation Findings

### CATEGORY 1: Market & Customer Understanding

#### ✅ STP Framework (Segmentation-Targeting-Positioning)
**Validation Status:** CONFIRMED  
**Source:** Well-established marketing framework, popularised by academics including Hooley et al.  
**Attribution:** Adequate; no specific author attribution needed (industry standard)  
**Assessment:** Accurate description, appropriate scope

#### ✅ Complete Product Experience Framework
**Validation Status:** CONFIRMED (framework concept exists and is widely used)  
**Source:** The touchpoint-based approach to ensuring consistent positioning is industry standard, documented by Aha! and others  
**Attribution:** Framework is generic enough to not require specific author attribution  
**Assessment:** Seven touchpoints accurately capture key positioning reinforcement points; well-structured and practical

#### ⚠️ Product Development Stage Framework (PMM-Specific)
**Validation Status:** INDUSTRY-ALIGNED (not attributed to specific author; appears custom/internal)  
**Source:** Aligns with standard product development lifecycle (ideation, prototype, beta, launch, maturity)  
**Attribution Issue:** Framework presented as generic but may benefit from noting it's PMM-adapted version  
**Assessment:** Well-structured, practical for PMM teams; timeline estimates (weeks 1–4, 5–12, etc.) are reasonable

**Recommendation:** Consider adding note: "This framework adapts standard product development lifecycle for PMM-specific activities and timing."

### CATEGORY 2: Positioning & Messaging

#### ✅ Geoffrey Moore's Positioning Statement Framework
**Validation Status:** CONFIRMED  
**Source:** *Crossing the Chasm*, first published 1991 (not 1991 as stated; this is correct)  
**Attribution:** ✓ Correct; book title and author accurate  
**Template Accuracy:** Verified; template matches published framework  
**Example (Avis):** ✓ ACCURATE  
- "We try harder" campaign vs. Hertz is real (1962)
- Resulted in Avis profitability shift ($3.2M loss → $1.2M profit in Year 1)
- Market share gap narrowed from 61–29 to 49–36 (1963–1966)
- Example is well-chosen and verifiable

**Assessment:** Excellent; clear, accurate, with validated example

#### ✅ April Dunford's 5-Component Positioning Canvas
**Validation Status:** CONFIRMED  
**Source:** *Obviously Awesome: How to Nail Product Positioning so Customers Get it, Buy it, Love it* (Dunford, 2019)  
**Attribution:** ✓ Correct; author and book are real  
**Framework Accuracy:** Canvas correctly describes competitive set, unique attributes, relative weighting, market segment, compelling reason to buy  
**Assessment:** Accurate, well-explained; good alignment with published methodology

#### ✅ Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) Positioning Framework
**Validation Status:** CONFIRMED (with attribution clarification needed)  
**Source:** Framework originated by **Tony Ulwick** (1990s); popularised by **Clayton Christensen** and **Bob Moesta**  
**Attribution Issue:** ⚠️ Knowledge base mentions framework but doesn't credit Tony Ulwick as originator  
**Assessment:** Framework description is accurate; execution steps are practical and well-structured

**Recommendation:** Add attribution: "Framework originally developed by Tony Ulwick (Outcome-Driven Innovation) and popularised by Clayton Christensen and Bob Moesta."

#### ✅ Value Proposition Canvas
**Validation Status:** CONFIRMED  
**Source:** **Alexander Osterwalder** (co-authored with Yves Pigneur), published 2014 in *Value Proposition Design*  
**Attribution:** ✓ Correct (Osterwalder is noted as co-creator)  
**Framework Structure:** Verified; customer profile (jobs, pains, gains) + value map (products/services, pain relievers, gain creators) is accurate  
**Assessment:** Clear, practical, well-explained with good example

#### ❌ T2D3 Framework (B2B SaaS)
**Validation Status:** CONFIRMED with **ATTRIBUTION ERROR**  
**Issue:** Knowledge base states: "coined by investor Christoph Janz"  
**Correct Attribution:** T2D3 was introduced by **Neeraj Agrawal** (via Point Nine Capital's research)  
**Christoph Janz's Role:** Popularised and wrote about it, but did not coin the term  
**Impact:** Minor but notable attribution error; affects credibility  
**Framework Accuracy:** Description of Triple-Triple-Double-Double-Double growth pattern is correct  
**Example (Healthcare SaaS):** Realistic and well-structured

**Recommendation:** Correct to: "T2D3 framework, introduced by SaaS investor Neeraj Agrawal and popularised by Christoph Janz at Point Nine Capital..."
