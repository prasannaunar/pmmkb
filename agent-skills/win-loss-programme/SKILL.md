# Win/loss programme

Win/loss is a standing quarterly practice, not a one-off investigation. Its
output is a small set of patterns with owners attached, not a transcript
archive.

Paths below are relative to the knowledge base root. The full entry is in
`frameworks/06-product-experience-adoption.md`; an extended walkthrough is in
`case-studies/win-loss-analysis.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Select the sample.** Ten to twenty recent wins and ten to twenty recent
   losses from the past 30 to 90 days. Mix deal sizes and segments so one
   account type does not skew the findings, and resist the pull towards
   whoever is easiest to reach.
2. **Run semi-structured interviews, not surveys**, so the interviewer can
   follow an interesting answer. Core questions:
   - What problems were you trying to solve?
   - What options did you evaluate, and how did you first hear about each?
   - What was the primary reason you chose us, or a competitor?
   - What almost stopped the deal happening at all?
   - What surprised you about our product compared with theirs?
   - Looking back, what could we have shown or explained earlier that would
     have changed your timeline or your decision?
3. **Code for patterns, not quotes.** A theme mentioned by one buyer is an
   anecdote; a theme mentioned by five or more is a pattern worth acting on.
   Record the count alongside every theme.
4. **Segment by decision-maker.** Finance buyers weight price and return,
   technical buyers weight architecture and scale, and line-of-business buyers
   weight ease of use. Analyse wins and losses separately by role so the
   resulting change targets the right buyer with the right argument.
5. **Route each finding to an owner with a deadline.** Positioning, messaging,
   pricing, roadmap priority, and the sales playbook are the five destinations.
   A finding with no owner stalls at the recommendation stage, which is where
   most win/loss programmes quietly die.
6. **Close the loop with sales within two weeks**, in a live session where reps
   can push back. Sales buy-in is what turns a finding into changed behaviour
   on live deals.
7. **Track across quarters.** Keep a running log so trends are visible: have
   loss reasons shifted, is the updated messaging landing, is a product gap
   widening or closing?

## Cadence and triggers

Run the full cycle quarterly as the standing rhythm, refreshing the sample each
time from the prior 30 to 90 days of closed deals. Run an off-cycle round when
a high-value account is lost, when win rate declines with no clear cause, or
ahead of a pricing or packaging change.

## Gotchas

- **Reps should not interview their own lost deals.** Buyers soften the real
  reason to avoid an awkward conversation, and the rep hears what confirms
  their existing account of the loss. Use PMM, a neutral internal interviewer,
  or a third party.
- **"Too expensive" is almost never a price finding.** It is usually a value
  communication finding. Probe what they compared the price against and what
  they expected to get for it before routing anything to pricing.
- **CRM closed-lost codes are not evidence.** Reps pick from a dropdown under
  time pressure at the end of a bad quarter. Use the codes to select the
  sample, never as the analysis.
- **Interviewing only losses halves the value.** Wins reveal which proof points
  actually moved the deal, which is exactly what messaging and battlecards
  need.
- **Do not act on a theme with fewer than five mentions.** Rewriting positioning
  on the strength of one articulate buyer is how a knowledge base of patterns
  becomes a record of loud opinions.
- **Recency matters.** A buyer interviewed six months after the decision
  reconstructs a tidier story than the one they lived. Stay inside the 30 to
  90 day window.

## Output

Deliver the interview guide used (`assets/interview-guide.md`), the sample
composition, the coded theme table with mention counts split by win and loss
and by buyer role, the routed findings with owners and dates, and the running
quarter-on-quarter trend log.
