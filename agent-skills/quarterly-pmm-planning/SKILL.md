# Quarterly PMM planning

A PMM quarter fails in one of two ways: it is a list of activities with no line
back to a business goal, or it is a list of commitments that exceeds the
people-weeks available. The plan has to survive both tests.

Paths below are relative to the knowledge base root. The full entry is in
`frameworks/07-strategy-planning.md`; a working template is in
`templates/quarterly-planning-template.md`.

## Procedure

1. **Get the business goals first-hand.** Ask the CEO, VP Sales, or CPO what
   the company is prioritising this quarter. Do not infer it from last
   quarter's plan; business goals move faster than PMM plans do, and a stale
   assumption invalidates everything downstream.
2. **Translate each business goal into PMM work, in writing.** Market entry
   becomes competitive positioning and market education. Revenue growth becomes
   persona-specific sales enablement. A launch becomes the go-to-market motion.
   Retention becomes customer education and upsell messaging. This translation
   table is the artefact that proves the quarter is business-connected rather
   than busywork; it is also what you show when the plan is questioned.
3. **Set two or three key results per goal**, each measurable, owned, and
   dated. "80% of reps complete positioning training by end of week 1, and 60%
   of new-product deals cite the messaging in sales notes by month 1" is a key
   result. "Improve enablement" is not. More than three per goal dilutes focus
   and makes weekly tracking unwieldy.
4. **Assess real capacity.** Count people-weeks net of holidays, all-hands
   events, and expected attrition. Estimate effort per initiative in
   people-weeks. Hold back 10 to 15% for unplanned requests; reactive sales
   asks and urgent competitive responses always arrive, and an unbuffered plan
   collapses at the first fire drill.
5. **Stack-rank by impact times confidence.** Score each initiative 1 to 3 on
   impact and 1 to 3 on confidence, multiply, and sort. Draw the resourcing cut
   line where capacity runs out.
6. **Publish the not-doing list.** Everything below the cut line is stated
   explicitly, with the reason. A silent omission becomes a mid-quarter
   surprise and, eventually, a reputation problem.
7. **Map handoffs before the quarter starts.** For each cross-functional
   initiative, name the counterpart, the artefact each side owes, and the date.
   Dependencies discovered when a deadline is missed are not dependencies, they
   are incidents.
8. **Run weekly check-ins and a quarterly retrospective.** Fifteen minutes on
   progress, blockers, adjustments. At quarter end, record what was achieved
   and what was learned, so the next plan starts from evidence rather than
   memory.

## When the quarter needs a strategic frame first

- **Choices above the operational level are unsettled** (where to play, how to
  win): run the Playing to Win cascade before allocating a single people-week.
  A quarterly plan cannot compensate for an unmade strategic choice.
- **Growth has stalled and nobody agrees why:** run the MOVE GTM diagnostic to
  find which handoff in the revenue engine is the binding constraint, then aim
  the quarter at that constraint rather than spreading effort evenly.
- **The plan does not fit the team no matter how it is cut:** that is a
  headcount case, not a planning problem. Use the PMM Team Scaling Framework to
  argue it with the initiative list and the not-doing list as evidence.

## Gotchas

- **Reactive work is the plan's largest line item and is usually unbudgeted.**
  If last quarter lost 30% of its capacity to unplanned requests, budget 30%
  this quarter and say so, rather than booking it at zero again.
- **Activity counts are not key results.** "Publish 12 blog posts" tracks
  effort; "60% of new-product deals cite the messaging" tracks whether the
  effort worked. Pick metrics with a stated baseline; see
  `measurement-guide.md` for the baseline metric, cadence, and owner per entry.
- **A launch calendar with eight Tier 1 launches is a resourcing problem, not
  an ambitious quarter.** Check the tier distribution from `launch-planning`
  before committing capacity.
- **Confidence is not enthusiasm.** Score confidence on evidence that the
  initiative will produce its stated result, not on how much the team wants to
  do it.
- **Mid-quarter reprioritisation is normal; a silent one is not.** When
  something is added, name what drops below the cut line to make room, in the
  same message.
- **Plans made without sales and product input are renegotiated in week three.**
  Circulate the stack-rank before it is final, not after.

## Output

Deliver the business-goal-to-PMM-work translation table, the OKRs with owners
and dates, the capacity model in people-weeks including the buffer, the ranked
initiative list with the cut line drawn, the explicit not-doing list, the
handoff map, and the weekly check-in and retrospective cadence. Use
`templates/quarterly-planning-template.md` as the working format.
