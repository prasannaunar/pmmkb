# Quiz: Ansoff Matrix

1. A marketing analytics company sells to e-commerce brands. Engineering proposes a new fraud detection product for banks, arguing it reuses 60% of the existing code base and is therefore "just product development". How should the PMM place this option?
   - **A.** As diversification: both a new product and a new market for the business.
   - **B.** As product development, with a new product sold to buyers who already know us.
   - **C.** As market development, with an existing product taken into the banking sector.
   - **D.** As market penetration, with growth built on code the team already maintains.

   **Correct answer: A.** Banks do not buy from the company today, and fraud detection is not a product it sells. Options are placed by what buyers and markets know about you, not by what engineering can reuse, so this carries diversification-level risk.

   *Why not B:* This is the near miss: it uses the right lens for one dimension, a new product, but wrongly treats banks as an existing market. Shared code does not make the buyer familiar with you.

   *Why not C:* Market development means taking an existing product to a new market. Fraud detection is a new product, so this label understates the risk.

   *Why not D:* Market penetration means selling more of an existing product to existing customers. Neither condition holds here.

2. Leadership agrees "we will pursue market development next year" at an offsite. Two quarters later nothing has moved. What is the most likely cause?
   - **A.** The direction was never turned into a named segment, a sized market and a test.
   - **B.** The direction was right, but the team should have run a full launch in every region.
   - **C.** Market development was too risky, and the team should have stayed with penetration.
   - **D.** The offsite chose correctly, but the sales team was not trained on the new product.

   **Correct answer: A.** A quadrant is a direction, not a plan. The entry asks every chosen option to carry a target market, a sizing estimate and a test with a success threshold, so the team knows what to do on Monday.

   *Why not B:* This is a near miss: it recognises an execution gap but proposes the wrong scope. Launching everywhere at once is the opposite of setting a focused segment and a cheap test.

   *Why not C:* Market development carries moderate risk and is often a sensible choice. The scenario shows a lack of specificity, not a wrong direction.

   *Why not D:* Market development uses an existing product, so there is no new product to train on. The gap is in defining the target market, not in sales training.

3. A CEO asks the PMM to help compare four growth options for the board. Which contribution best fits PMM's role?
   - **A.** Sizing each target market and setting out the customer evidence behind each option.
   - **B.** Choosing the winning option and presenting it to the board as PMM's own decision.
   - **C.** Building the five-year financial model for each option with the company's auditors.
   - **D.** Estimating each option's engineering cost and delivery timeline for the product team.

   **Correct answer: A.** PMM supplies the evidence that sets each option's real risk and reward: market sizing, customer research and competitive analysis. The choice of direction belongs to the CEO and leadership team.

   *Why not B:* This is the near miss: PMM can frame the options and recommend one, but the decision belongs to leadership. Presenting it as PMM's decision confuses contribution with ownership.

   *Why not C:* Financial modelling belongs to finance. PMM's sizing feeds the model, but the model itself is not PMM's deliverable.

   *Why not D:* Engineering estimates come from product and engineering. PMM contributes market and customer evidence, not build costs.

4. A project management company's growth has slowed in its core agency market. Leadership wants to sell the existing product to in-house marketing teams. Interviews show the problem is the same but the buyer is a marketing operations lead, not an agency owner. What should the PMM prepare first?
   - **A.** New positioning and segment messaging for in-house teams, tested in a pilot.
   - **B.** A product development launch plan for existing agency accounts and their owners.
   - **C.** A price cut for agencies to win back share in the core market before expanding.
   - **D.** The same agency messaging, sent to in-house teams through the current campaigns.

   **Correct answer: A.** This is market development: an existing product in a new market. Its main unknowns are whether the new buyer responds to the positioning, so fresh positioning and segment messaging, tested in a pilot, is the right first step.

   *Why not B:* Product development means a new product for existing customers. This option keeps the product the same and changes the market, so a product development plan targets the wrong buyers.

   *Why not C:* A price cut is a market penetration tactic in the core market. It does not address the in-house option leadership chose.

   *Why not D:* This is the near miss: it targets the right segment, but reuses positioning written for a different buyer. Market development usually needs new positioning, references and messaging.

5. A team presents three growth options: a premium tier for current customers, a new region for the current product, and a new product for a new industry. They propose putting the whole year's budget on the new industry because "it has the biggest market". What should the PMM recommend?
   - **A.** Combine a low-risk penetration push with a tested moderate-risk move, before a bet.
   - **B.** Fund the new industry fully, but run a concept test in the background as a safeguard.
   - **C.** Drop the new industry and the new region, putting everything into the premium tier.
   - **D.** Fund all three options equally, so the business does not miss any of the openings.

   **Correct answer: A.** The entry recommends a portfolio of moves: one low-risk penetration push plus one or two moderate-risk moves, each with a cheap test before full investment. Staking the year on diversification puts the whole plan on the option with the least evidence.

   *Why not B:* This is the near miss: it includes a test, but in the wrong sequence. A test only protects the business if the result decides the investment, not if it runs after the money is committed.

   *Why not C:* Avoiding all risk caps growth at the size of the current market. The entry recommends balancing a safe move with moderate ones, not avoiding them altogether.

   *Why not D:* Splitting budget evenly ignores the different risks and evidence behind each option. It underfunds the safer moves and still overfunds the riskiest.
