# Quiz: Adjacent Disciplines

1. A CEO is preparing next year's plan for a company with four products. Each product team is asking for more budget, and the CEO wants one picture showing which products fund growth and which need a decision. Which tool fits best?
   - **A.** The BCG Growth-Share Matrix, plotting each product by market growth and share.
   - **B.** The Ansoff Matrix, sorting each product's growth options by product and market.
   - **C.** Porter's Five Forces, rating each product's market on the five competitive forces.
   - **D.** The Business Model Canvas, mapping each product's nine blocks in turn on a page.

   **Correct answer: A.** The growth-share matrix is built for splitting investment across a portfolio. It shows which products generate cash and which consume it, and which question marks need an invest-or-exit decision.

   *Why not B:* This is the near miss: Ansoff is also a leadership growth tool, but it sorts growth options, not existing products. It answers "where should growth come from next?", not "how do we split budget across what we have?"

   *Why not C:* Five forces judges how attractive one market is. Running it four times would not show how the products balance each other's cash needs.

   *Why not D:* The canvas maps how one business model works. It does not compare products against each other for investment.

2. Growth in a company's core market has slowed. Leadership is debating a premium tier for current customers, a push into a new industry, and a brand-new product. They want to see the options side by side with their risk made explicit. Which tool fits best?
   - **A.** The Ansoff Matrix, placing each option by new or existing product and market.
   - **B.** The BCG Growth-Share Matrix, placing each option by market growth and share.
   - **C.** The Business Model Canvas, drawing a full canvas for each of the three options.
   - **D.** Cialdini's Principles of Persuasion, testing which option buyers find appealing.

   **Correct answer: A.** Ansoff sorts growth options into penetration, market development, product development and diversification. Risk rises with distance from what the company already knows, which is exactly the comparison leadership wants.

   *Why not B:* This is the near miss: the growth-share matrix is a portfolio tool, but it plots products that already exist and have a measurable share. Options that have not been launched have no share to plot.

   *Why not C:* A canvas per option can follow once a direction is chosen, but it does not compare options by risk.

   *Why not D:* Persuasion principles shape how messaging works on buyers. They do not compare strategic growth options.

3. A company is considering entering the small accounting firm market. The founders want to know whether the market can support healthy margins, not just whether firms have the problem. Which tool fits best?
   - **A.** Porter's Five Forces, rating rivals, entrants, substitutes, buyers and suppliers.
   - **B.** The BCG Growth-Share Matrix, plotting the new market next to the existing portfolio.
   - **C.** TAM/SAM/SOM Market Sizing, estimating the number of firms and what each would pay.
   - **D.** The Ansoff Matrix, classifying the move as market development or diversification.

   **Correct answer: A.** Five forces judges how much of the value a market creates its companies can keep. That is the margin question the founders are asking.

   *Why not B:* The growth-share matrix needs a share in the market, which the company does not yet have. It does not assess margin potential.

   *Why not C:* This is the near miss: market sizing tells you how big the prize is, but not how much profit the market structure allows. The two are complementary, and the founders asked about margins.

   *Why not D:* Ansoff classifies the move and its risk, but it does not assess the market's profit potential.

4. Leadership wants to add a self-serve tier, sold online with monthly payments, to a product currently sold through direct sales on annual contracts. They want to see what else in the business must change. Which tool fits best?
   - **A.** The Business Model Canvas, drawing the new tier's model as its own canvas.
   - **B.** The Ansoff Matrix, classifying the new tier as product or market development.
   - **C.** Porter's Five Forces, rating the self-serve market's competitive structure.
   - **D.** The Value Proposition Canvas, mapping jobs, pains and gains for the new tier.

   **Correct answer: A.** The Business Model Canvas shows how a change in one block, such as channels or revenue streams, ripples through customer relationships and cost structure. A separate canvas for the new tier makes the knock-on changes visible.

   *Why not B:* Ansoff names the type of growth move but not the operational changes it requires. Leadership asked what else must change.

   *Why not C:* Five forces rates market structure. It does not map the internal changes a new revenue model needs.

   *Why not D:* This is the near miss: the Value Proposition Canvas comes from the same team, but it zooms in on two blocks only. Channels, revenue and costs sit outside it.

5. A company's free trial converts poorly. The homepage shows logos from much larger companies than the target buyers, and the pricing page has a discount timer that resets weekly. Which tool best structures a review?
   - **A.** Cialdini's Principles of Persuasion, auditing proof, scarcity and commitment.
   - **B.** Porter's Five Forces, checking whether buyer power is what suppresses conversion.
   - **C.** The Business Model Canvas, reviewing the channels and relationships blocks.
   - **D.** Message Testing, comparing two new homepage headlines in an A/B experiment.

   **Correct answer: A.** The persuasion principles give a structured audit of where an asset uses, misuses or misses each lever. Here, social proof from dissimilar companies and fake scarcity are the obvious problems.

   *Why not B:* Buyer power describes market structure, not why one trial journey underperforms. The issues here are on the page itself.

   *Why not C:* The canvas maps the business model at a high level. It would not surface specific problems with proof and urgency on a page.

   *Why not D:* This is the near miss: testing is a sound later step, but testing new headlines leaves the flawed proof and fake timer in place. The audit comes first.

6. A PMM supports a portfolio review. The CEO plans to divest a product with low share in a flat market, but the PMM's research suggests a different segment values it highly. What should the PMM do?
   - **A.** Raise the repositioning option and propose a cheap test before divesting.
   - **B.** Accept the dog label and let the divestment go ahead on the agreed timeline.
   - **C.** Overrule the CEO's decision and keep investing in the product from PMM budget.
   - **D.** Stay silent and leave the decision to the CEO and the strategy team alone.

   **Correct answer: A.** The growth-share matrix warns against treating the quadrant as destiny. PMM's role is to bring the customer evidence that could change the picture and suggest a cheap test, while leaving the decision with the CEO.

   *Why not B:* Labelling a product a dog often becomes self-fulfilling once investment stops. The PMM has evidence that should be heard first.

   *Why not C:* This is the near miss: it acts on the evidence, but takes a decision PMM does not own. Contributing evidence is PMM's role; overruling leadership is not.

   *Why not D:* PMM's role in this model is to supply market and customer evidence. Withholding relevant evidence fails that role.

7. Which statement best describes how PMM relates to the entries in the Adjacent Disciplines area?
   - **A.** PMM supplies evidence and applies the result; another function owns the call.
   - **B.** PMM owns each decision outright once the company grows past its founding stage.
   - **C.** PMM has no role in them, and they are included only as background reading.
   - **D.** PMM runs them as standard PMM methods, in the same way as the core entries.

   **Correct answer: A.** Adjacent entries come from neighbouring disciplines. The decisions they feed belong to the CEO, CFO, strategy or other functions, and each entry's "PMM's role" section sets out what PMM contributes and how it applies the result.

   *Why not B:* Ownership of portfolio, growth and business model decisions moves towards leadership and strategy teams as companies scale, not towards PMM.

   *Why not C:* PMM has a clear contributing role in each, which is why they are in the knowledge base at all.

   *Why not D:* This is the near miss: PMM does sometimes build these analyses, but they are not methods PMM runs as its own, which is what separates them from core entries.

8. A PMM reviews a five forces analysis that lists competitors, customers and suppliers with no judgement on their strength. A colleague suggests adding a SWOT. What is the better fix?
   - **A.** Rate each force with evidence and conclude on overall market attractiveness.
   - **B.** Add the SWOT, so internal strengths and weaknesses sit beside the external view.
   - **C.** Replace the analysis with a BCG matrix, so the review focuses on market share.
   - **D.** Add more competitors and suppliers, so the list covers every party in the market.

   **Correct answer: A.** A five forces analysis only supports a decision when each force is rated and the forces are judged together. A SWOT tends to produce lists in the same way, which is why this knowledge base does not carry it as an entry.

   *Why not B:* This is the near miss: it adds an internal view, but a SWOT would not supply the missing ratings. Judgement, not another list, is the gap.

   *Why not C:* The BCG matrix answers a different question about portfolio investment. It does not assess market structure.

   *Why not D:* More names make a longer list without a conclusion. The problem is the missing judgement.

9. Leadership chooses market development: selling the existing product to a new industry. What should PMM prepare next?
   - **A.** Positioning and segment messaging for the new industry, piloted first.
   - **B.** A product development launch plan for current customers and their teams.
   - **C.** A new Business Model Canvas, mapped before any customer research is done.
   - **D.** A persuasion audit of the current homepage, aimed at existing customers.

   **Correct answer: A.** Market development keeps the product and changes the buyer. Its main unknowns are whether the new segment responds to the positioning, so new segment messaging and a pilot are PMM's first job.

   *Why not B:* Product development means a new product for existing customers. This plan targets the wrong market.

   *Why not C:* This is the near miss: a canvas can help, but filling it in without customer evidence records beliefs. The first need is evidence from the new segment.

   *Why not D:* A homepage audit for existing customers does not address the new industry leadership chose.

10. A strategy team defines its industry as "all enterprise software globally" for an analysis of market profitability. The output is too vague to act on. Which tool is being used, and what went wrong?
   - **A.** Porter's Five Forces, with an industry defined so broadly it blurs forces.
   - **B.** The BCG Growth-Share Matrix, with a market defined to flatter relative share.
   - **C.** The Ansoff Matrix, with options placed by code reuse, not market knowledge.
   - **D.** The Business Model Canvas, with several business models mapped on one page.

   **Correct answer: A.** Analysing market profitability is the job of five forces, and Porter warned that defining the industry too broadly or narrowly is the commonest error. The method starts with one product market and geography.

   *Why not B:* This is the near miss: market definition is also the main risk in the BCG matrix, but there it distorts share, not profitability. The scenario describes an industry profitability analysis.

   *Why not C:* Ansoff misplacement concerns growth options, not industry profitability.

   *Why not D:* Mixing business models is a canvas pitfall, but the canvas does not assess market profitability.
